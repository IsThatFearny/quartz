### Was long-distance travel basically a matter of setting out the unknown during the Middle Ages?

Knowledge of peoples and cultures (however vague and inaccurate) but lack of geographical literacy until the 13th & 14th centuries

Dependent on which routes one was travelling - pilgrimages were well-trod with various resting points along the way

Travel guidance was practical rather than relying on cartographic accuracy

Cosmography was a major source of information, but this was inaccessible to many; likewise, many were illiterate and could not read travel journals

Diplomatic relationships existed, but not in the modern sense, nor were they very strong - an awareness existed, but not true knowledge

Advances in cartography did eventually come (see Catalan Atlas)

Western cartography was relatively ignorant of the east (again see Catalan Atlas and Hereford Map)
