# Medieval Worlds Lecture 13 - The East Roman World


- Only the 'western' part of the Roman Empire fell in the late 5th-century - the 'east' (i.e. modern day Turkey, Georgia, Egypt, etc.) continues
- Unbroken institutional continuity from the fall of Rome to the Crusades
- Constantinople (i.e. Istanbul) became the 'new Rome'
- East Roman empire is able to reconquer areas of the western Roman Empire (such as Italy, south Spain, etc.) by 555 AD
- In the 7th-century, 2/3 of the East Roman Empire is conquered by the Islamic Caliphates
- Large-scale militarisation of democracy, and by extension, society
- Elite competition is for titles with salaries, rather than land (as in the Medieval West)
- Imperial administration was incredibly complex; likewise, the church had a very complex power structure
- Much territory was lost to the Normans, Seljuk Turks, etc, leaving the empire limited only to those areas surrounding the Aegean and Black Sea; eventually in the 13th-century, the East Roman Empire fragmented into successor states

### Continuity or Rupture?

- There was a continuous state between 395 and 1204, with the same imperial institutions remaining throughout
- However, in the political economy, society, and class structure, there was major fragmentation
