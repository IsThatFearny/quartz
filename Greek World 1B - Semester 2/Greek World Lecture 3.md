#### The Parthenon

- Dates to c. 447-432BC
- Presumed to be a temple to Athena (manifestation named *Athena Parthenos*, hence the name)
	- N.B. presumed as we have no evidence of dedications to Athena Parthenos, nor do we know the location of her altar
- Designed by Iktinos and Kallikrates; the chief sculptor was Pheidias (with many other sculptors working below him)
- Built with Pentelic marble (local Athenian marble)
- 'Peristyle' columns, making the Parthenon a 'Peripteral building'
- One would traditionally enter the Parthenon from the East
- Larger room was called the *cella*; the other room, separated entirely from it, named the *adyton*.
- The East entrance (porch) was named the *opisthodomos*; the West entrance was named the *pronaos*
- Number of columns on the long side are double +1 the number on the short side - this was considered the 'ideal' number
- A combination of doric (in all columns but the four in the adyton) and ionic (the four columns in the Adyton, which have a base - although there is debate over whether they are ionic or corinthian) architecture, unlike the vast majority of contemporary Greek temples
- Multiple temples were built on the site before Parthenon - it is the third iteration of the temple (see below)

- No straight lines used in the Parthenon - this is because it is built so that the floor curves upwards towards the centre, so that the highest point is in this spot (the same is true for the roof)
- All columns incline slightly inwards - at the corners they incline inwards *diagonally*
- Three lines alternating with squares in the entablature of the building -  a *doric frieze*. Each *metope* (square) was carved with a different theme, which is unique amongst all Greek temples
- Each side of the building depicted a different theme in the metopes - on the East side of the building was depicted Athena's victory; on the west side, there was a depiction of the battle which took place at the acropolis between the Greeks and the Amazons (a race of warrior women) as a result of Thesus' kidnapping of an Amazon queen; the north side is themed around the fall of Troy; and the south side has the theme of the Centauromacy, i.e. the fight between the Greeks and the Centaurs as a result of the centaur's assault of the Lapith women
- The south metopes survived better than the other three sides, as they were positioned on the side of the Parthenon by which it was significantly more difficult to intentionally damage (e.g. Ottoman soldiers using them as target practise)

#### The Parthenon Frieze

- The Parthenon frieze wraps around the entire building (the 'inner' exterior). We are missing around 20% of it (100ft out of approx. 500ft). The story that it depicts (chronologically) begins at the south-west corner. The frieze was painted and had bronze extrusions (such as the reins of horses)
- Two maxims - 'nothing in excess' and 'know thyself' - apply well the frieze
- The frieze is not well-scaled (the horses are too small for the riders)
- As the frieze continues, the action decreases - less horses are present, people are on foot, and women are introduced more frequently
- The south side depicts restless animals being brought to slaughter/sacrifice in a religious procession
- Action stops almost completely on the east side - figures are standing or sitting. Those who stand are women manipulating a piece of cloth - a peplos, a woman's article of clothing - as an offering to Athena (or at least her manifestation in the form of a statue). The gods surround these women, with their backs turned; all are identifiable by their placement and depiction.
