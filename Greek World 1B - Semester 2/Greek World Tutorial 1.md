1. Who is Pericles? Provide some biographical facts about him.

Pericles:

- Son of Xanthippos, an Athenian general
- Lived in the 5th century
- A prominent Athenian politican
- Served as a general several times, raiding various cities and islands and continuously getting elected to the position over 15 years
- Heavily involved in the outbreak and subsequent waging of the Peloponnesian war - his refusal to rescind a trade decree is largely seen as a cause for the war - and during the war he advocated a defensive strategy, fortifying Athens rather than pushing outwards

2. What is meant by a Funeral Speech?  

Fallen soldiers in Athens were given a public burial at a specific cemetery after the battle in which they were killed. At the burial, a man would give a funeral oration, a sort of eulogy; this was generally a man who was known to be a good orator, a respectable man, and a skilled leader (such as Pericles). It is important to note that this is a distinctly Athenian tradition, as other Greek societies buried their dead on the battlefield - the Athenians departed from this.

1. How does this funeral speech differ from earlier funeral speeches? No earlier  
speeches survive - work it out from this one.  

It would seem the Pericles has a disdain for the funeral speech, stating that his words alone are not enough to bestow the honour which the fallen deserve. He sees it as a requirement of the Athenian constitution, rather than an important burial ritual. He makes the point that the funeral oration is not taken by any man as truth, but as either an under-statement or an exaggeration. He states that his predecessors did not take such a view, praising the man who first began the tradition.
