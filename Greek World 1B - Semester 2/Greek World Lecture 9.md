- Sparta was largely devoid of grandiose buildings, temples, etc. (Thucydides says that, were Sparta abandoned, one would find it difficult to credit its reputation for being a powerful city-state; this is in contrast to Athens, where one would credit it as having twice the power as it did). The city was a collection of villages in the traditional Greek way
- Sparta had a policy of secrecy, keeping the numbers of their army and the extent of their power hidden so that their enemies could not predict their strength
- The 'Spartan Mirage' deceived enemies into believing that Spartan strength was much larger than it may have been in reality - e.g. combing their hair before a battle as a ritual, to exude an image of collectedness and calm
- Spartans had a specific education system which would allow their elite soldiers to prevent themselves as the bravest and toughest soldiers on earth
- Despite unrest in Spartan politics, the system of good order and their constitution had been in place for over 400 years without interference, which allowed for them to intervene in the affairs of other states without fearing political turmoil at home
- Lycurgus (a Spartan lawgiver) was allegedly taught constitutional reform by the Oracle of Delphi - he changed their military policy and governmental process, importing the constitution from Crete
- We know that the Spartan constitution dates to at least the 7th century BC, but know no more specifics than this

### Organisation of Spartan politics and society

**Political institutions:**  

• Two kings (allegedly descendants of Herakles; families called Agiadai and Eurypontidai)  
• Senate (gerousia): 28 Spartiates > 60 years old (members for life) + 2 kings  
• Assembly (apella): All the Spartiates > 18 years old.  
• Five Ephors: oversee the conduct of the kings.  

**Organisation of society:**  

• Spartiates or Homoioi (“The Similars”): full citizens and warrior class.  
• Perioeci (“Dwellers of the surroundings”): Lakedaimonians of surrounding territory; not full citizens.  
• Helots: conquered and eslaved subjects, lived in Eurotas valley and in Messenia.

</br>

- Sparta had various problems with their kings and generals
	- Demeratos was exiled and fled to Persia in 491
	- Cleomenes was exiled, subsequently recalled, and then suffered a violent death as a prisoner in Sparta in 490
	- Pausanius was accused of tyranny and Medism during a campaign against Byzantium, and put to death
	- etc.

- Sparta did not feel safe in their home territory, because they were constantly at war with their neighbours in the Peloponnese - they fought with Arcadia, Argolis, Laconia, and Messenia
