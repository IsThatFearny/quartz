### When did the Delian League turn into an empire? (Continued from Lecture 1)

Harold Mattingly makes the argument that the league became an empire between Pericles and Cleon (basing his argument on the writings of Thucydides). Pericles was a more moderate leader, whereas Cleon was an imperialist demagogue

#### Evolution of Language

In various decrees throughout this period, there was a clear change in the language used to describe the league/developing empire: from 'the alliance' to 'Athens and her allies', and then to 'the cities that the Athenians rule'

#### The Three Bar Sigma Controversy

The Three Bar Sigma was originally thought to date a source as pre-445 BC (as from 445 BC onwards a four bar sigma was used). However, this theory was proven false in 1990, showing that:

- Epigraphic features cannot be treated as decisive in establishing a chronology
- All decrees featuring 'harsh' imperialistic language may need to be downdated to the era of Cleon
- Imperial activities were a reality during the age of Pericles, and even earlier, e.g. oaths of loyalty, Athenian intervention in external politics, suppression of dissent, etc.

</br>

The rule of Pericles was generally harsh, and there was a turn amongst the Greeks towards Sparta, who had promised liberation. It became impossible for Athens to dissolve the empire without incurring the wrath of those who felt that they had been wronged or suppressed by the Athenians.

### Athenian Democracy

#### Main Sources

- *The Athenaion Politeia* of the School of Aristotle
- *The Athenian Constitution* by the 'Old Oligarch'
- Attic orators, mainly 4th century
- Attic inscriptions

</br>

#### Development of Democracy

- Solon: archonship 594 BC
- The reforms of Cleisthenes: 510-508 BC
- The introduction of ostracism: 488 BC
- The reforms of Ephialtes: 462 BC
- Reconciliation and revision of the law code: 403 BC

</br>

Athens changed their constitution **eleven** times before 'democracy' came into being

##### The Reforms of Cleisthenes

Cleisthenes introduced a legislative body, the **Council of 500**. It included 50 members per tribe. A form of devolution was also introduced: 10 tribes, each with 3 trittyes (larger regions; one city, one coastal, and one inland); and 139 demes (village/local community equivalents), distributed across the trittyes.

##### The introduction of Ostracism

Ostracism could be used against anyone who had too much power/was a tyrant. It was introduced to ostracise the friends of the tyrants. Ostracism was an act that was voted upon, requiring a majority to carry out.

##### The Reforms of Ephialtes

The Areopagus was originally an oligarchic judiciary council, who claimed to be 'guardians of the constitution'. Ephialtes stripped the council of the majority of its powers in 462 BC, and it served mainly as a homicide court, expanding democracy further and separating the legislature from the state/those in power.

##### Reconciliation agreement and revision of the law code

The reconciliation agreement placed the safety of Athens above the settlement of private rivalries, and the democrats and oligarchs agreed to forget past disagreements. A commission of twenty was elected to govern Athens until the law codes could be revised - during this time the law code written up by Solon and the statutes introduced by Draco were used.

</br>

##### System of law-making

Each tribe would serve as prytany (presidents of the council), for 36 days each. They set the agenda for the council's proceedings, where said agenda would have to be decided upon by the majority before any action could be taken.

##### Officers

Officials for an administration were appointed by allotment, aside from the treasurer of the stratiotic fund and those in charge of the theoric fund, who were elected. War officials were also elected.

Similarly, bureaucrats (*logistai*) were also allotted from councillors in order to scrutinise accounts and the actions of various officers - they did this ten times throughout a calendar year. Also allotted were *euthynoi* and 2 *paredroi* per *euthynoi*, who were responsible for handling complaints and accusations against officers.

</br>

##### Judiciary

Solon gave all citizens the right to prosecute their fellow in the event of wrongdoing. He also introduced a right of appeal in the lawcourts.

Lawcourt juries were allotted by nine archones, tribes, and the secretary of the *thesmothetai* for the tenth tribe. Jurors were divided yet again by a complex allotment system to decide which jurors would serve on a particular case.

All participants had to swear oaths, speeches were timed by water clocks. In the case that jury votes were equally divided, the defendant would win. Penalties were often monetary, or involved the deprivation of freedom.

</br>

##### Citizenship

Citizenship was granted if one met the following conditions:

- Male
- Born of two citizen parents
- Registered when 18
- Free
- Voted for by demesmen

There is a major distinction between *astoi* (citizens by birth) and *politai* (political rights holders). The former included women and defined citizenship of the state, as opposed to the latter which gave one political rights.
