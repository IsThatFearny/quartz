- Empire is a 'lived formation'/'world-building', not something that exists solely geographically
- It is the way that people are governed, classified, protected, excluded, etc.
- Legal empires is about how imperial power is organised, justified, and naturalised
- Law is constructed, culturally/socially specific, and emerges from particular histories
- Slavery
	- Legal basis on which to dehumanise
	- Drew a distinction between governors and governed
- Law rationalises violence, turning conquest into administration - characterises subjugated populations as deficient and in need of ruling
- Law also becomes key for sites of resistance (e.g. legal changes as a result of petitions, direct action, etc.)
- How does law make empire normal, necessary, and inevitable?
