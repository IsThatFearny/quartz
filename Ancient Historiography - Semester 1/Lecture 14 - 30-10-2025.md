### Josephus' Works

- *Bellum Judaicum* (The Jewish War)
- *Antiquitates Judaicae* (The Jewish Antiquities)
- *Contra Apionem* (Against Apion)
- *Vita* (Life)

**Josephus referencing conventions**

- Whiston: A.J. 18. 1. 2.
	- Abbreviated title, book, chapter, section
- Niese: A.J. 18. 278
	- Abbreviated title, book, section
- Whiston may be used in older literature - Niese tends to be the system used in contemporary works

**When referencing classical sources, use in-text citations**

</br>

#### *Vita*

- In Greek
- Written in 90s CE (approx.)
- Shortest work
- Earliest autobiography that survives from antiquity - a defence of Josephus' own actions

</br>

#### *Bellum Judaicum*

- Written in 7 books
- Account of the Jewish Revolt (began 66 CE)
- Written in Greek in c. 75 CE (although there is reference to an original written in Aramaic)
- Josephus was involved in the events which he records - argues that the Jewish revolt was caused by Roman misgovernment and by a few 'bad apples' in the Jewish community (and not Jews like Josephus himself)

</br>

#### Audience

- Wrote for sympathetic or tolerant audience in Rome who had an elite education
- Especially wrote for Greeks and Romans (although also for some Judeans in Rome)

</br>

#### Destruction of the Temple

- Not thought well of to destroy the house of a deity (even if not Roman)
- Josephus treads a fine line: has to avoid offending the elites (the emperor and his lineage) whilst also providing a relatively accurate account
- Uses ambiguous language and appeals to divine intervention to achieve this:


> [!quote] Josephus, BJ 6.249-253
> "God had for certain long ago doomed it to the fire; and now that fatal day was come"
> "although these flames took their rise from the Jews themselves"
> "being hurried on by a certain divine fury"


- Attempts to work himself into certain historiographical models

</br>

#### Classical Comparanda

- Compares himself with Thucydides and others, criticising their methdology - 'the rising tide of sycophancy deterred them'; 'falsified through cowardace'; 'my design... [is] without anger and without partiality, from the motives of which I stand sufficiently removed'
- Positions himself as a Jew and uses this to give authority to his account
</br>

#### Antiquitates

- 20 books - echoing Dionysus of Halicarnassus
- Covers the history of the Jewish people from the biblical period right up until the Jewish war
- Books 1-10: biblical history
- Books 11-20: up until the Jewish war
- Writes a disproportionate amount about Herod - likely because he had good sources for him
