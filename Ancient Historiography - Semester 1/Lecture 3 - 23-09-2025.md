### Herodotus

- 'Father of Historiography' - Cicero
- From Halicarnassos (Turkey)
- Lived in the 5<sup>th</sup> century BCE
- Son of Lyxes and Dyro, related to the mythical founder of Ionia, Panyassis
- Exiled by Lygdamis to Samos, where he learned the Ionian dialect of Greek which he used in his *Histories*
- Subsequently returned to Halicarnassos and helped expel Lygdamis
- Exiled again in c. 447 CBE to Athens
- Assumed (although debated) that he travelled throughout the Eastern Mediterranean to Thrace, Phoenicia, Egypt, Mesopotamia, and Southern Italy
- Died at an uncertain date either at Athens, Thurioio or Pella in Macedonia

</br>

Herodotus' works were written at an important time in Greek history - there was a significant dispersal of Greek throughout the Med as a result of various economic factors, political instability, civil disagreements, and 'divine interventions'. It was also a time of significant cultural exchange and social mobility

</br>

> [!quote] Hom. Pd. 6.119-126, trans. adapted from S. Butler
> "Alas," said he to himself, "what kind of people have I come amongst? **Are they cruel, savage, and don't know laws, or hospitable and god-fearing?** I seem to hear the voices of young women, and they sound like those of the nymphs that haunt mountaintops, or springs of rivers and meadows of green grass. At any rate I am among a race of men and women. Let me try if I cannot manage to get a look at them"

The questions in bold were regularly asked of new communities which Herodotus came across - they were the main criteria for deeming a society 'civilised'

### Herodotus' *Histories*

</br>


> [!quote] Herodotus. 1.1, trans. A.D. Godley
> "This is the display of the inquiry of Herodotus of Halicarnassus, so that things done by man not be forgotten in time, and that great and marvellous deeds, some displayed by the Hellenes, some by the barbarians, not lose their glory, including among others what was the cause of their waging war on each other"

</br>

**Book I:** From Croesus to Cambyses (555-526 BCE)

**Book II:** Description of Egypt

**Book III:** From Cambyses to Darius (525-522 BCE)

**Book IV:** Against Scythia and Libya (522-513 BCE)

**Book V:** Against Thracia, Ionian Revolt (512-500 BCE)

**Book VI:** From the Revolt to Marathon (499-490 BCE)

**Book VII:** Xerxes' Campaign; Thermopylae (489-480 BCE)

**Book VIII:** Artermision; Salamis (480 BCE)

**Book IX:** Plataeae; Persian retreat (479 BCE)

### Mythos, Logos, and Historia

#### Mythos

word, tale, story, or narration, often associated with poets/the distant past. Sometimes contrasted with 'truth' but not always entirely false.

#### Logos

A speech, argument, account, or 'verified' tale/work, including rational elements. Neutral word: both fictional and factual. Often associated with the present or recent past

#### Historia

Research, 'scientific' inquiry, narration

</br>

Herodotus primarily found information of his subject matter through accounts told to him or through his own personal experience:


> [!quote] Herotodus. 2.99.1
> "So far, all I have said is the record of my own autopsy and judgment and inquiry (ὄψις τε ἐμὴ καὶ γνώμη καὶ ἱστορίη). Henceforth I will record Egyptian chronicles, according to what I have heard, adding something of what I myself have seen”
