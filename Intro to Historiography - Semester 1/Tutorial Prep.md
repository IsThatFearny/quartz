Previous historians dismissed a medieval concept of childhood primarily due to the arguments made by Philippe Aries, who claimed that the Middle Ages did not recognize a distinct life stage of childhood. He suggested that children were depicted in art as little adults and that there was no specific language to describe children, leading to the conclusion that the modern definitions of childhood and adolescence were invented later. Aries's reliance on evidence from sixteenth- and seventeenth-century writers, while neglecting earlier sources, contributed to this dismissal. His conclusions were seen as problematic by medieval and Renaissance scholars, who criticized his careless use of evidence and argued that he did not adequately consider the sources available from the medieval period. This criticism spurred further research into the concept of childhood during the Middle Ages, leading scholars to argue for the existence of a recognized life stage of childhood distinct from adolescence and adulthood (p. 440, p. 441).
