- What are the primary sources utilised in Peacock's text to document early Seljuq history? When were those texts composed? Are they all contemporary to Seljuq rule?  

Peacock uses focuses on the *Tarikh-i Mas 'udi*, written by the historian Bayhaqi and composed at the beginning of Seljuq rule (i.e. 1030-1040 CE);[^1] Gardizi's *Zayn al-Akhbar*, written in 1051 CE, merely a few years after the Seljuq rise to power;[^2] Sibt b. al-Jawzi's *Mir'at al-Zaman*, a thirteenth century text (although largely composed of the testimony of an 11th century historian);[^3] and Isfahani's *Nusrat al-fatra*, much of the text deriving largely from memoirs written in c. 1133 CE.[^4] 

The former two sources were written contemporary to Seljuq rule. However, the latter two were written after the height of the Seljuq empire (although the *Nusrat al-fatra* was written pre-dissolution of the empire as a whole). Despite this, the *Mir'at al-Zaman* is composed largely of primary sources which *were* contemporary to the time.
