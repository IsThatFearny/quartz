</br>

### Settled Societies
</br>

- Settled societies were largely agrarian, and the state's primary source of wealth was agriculture
- All sources from these societies were written by the elites (top 10% of the population) and by men
- Travel over land consisted of caravans, and by sea the primary medium was coastal navigation

</br>

### Nomadic Societies

- Nomadic societies were largely tribal, organised around a common ancestor (whether real or apocryphal)
- They generally have no written history (aside from the occasional mention in Greek, Persian, or Chinese sources)
- Thus, these societies are mostly known from the archaeological record
- Nomadic and semi-nomadic civilisation was very important for trade with the settled world:
	- The Turks traded cattle, slaves, and furs
	- The Berbers and Arabs usually traded spices
- However, they were also known as raiders, attacking the settled world

</br>

- Islamic settlements were not always in the desert; there were cities surrounding the Syrian desert and in South Arabia, and various oases throughout Arabia
- Arabia became a new geostrategic centre
	- Mecca became a centre of pilgrimage and a trading centre
	- Medina (the place of death of the prophet) also became a centre of pilgrimage

</br>

### The Prophet Muhammad

c. 570: born in Mecca into the Quraysh tribe and the Hashim clan, growing up as an orphan

c. 610: Received the first revelation

622: Muhammad and his followers emigrate from Mecca to Yathrib (Medina)

632: Muhammad dies in Medina

</br>

- Muhammad is the Prophet, religious and political leader of the nascent polity of the Muslim community
- After Muhammad died, the leadership of the Muslim community fell to the Caliphs, who were seen as his successors on earth
- The caliphate was briefly selective (first four successors), but in 661 it became hereditary, allowing the establishment of dynasties (e.g. Umayyads and 'Abbasids)
