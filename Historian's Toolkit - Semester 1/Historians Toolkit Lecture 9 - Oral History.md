# Historians Toolkit Lecture 9 - Oral History


- Oral history can refer, in the singular, to history recorded through spoken records; or in the plural, to a historical methodology involving the use of the spoken word
- Oral history is often highly useful in recording histories of illiterate societies or those who are unable to record their own histories
- There are, however, some criticisms of oral histories:
	- Oral history is very subjective - written documents are more objective and therefore better and of more value to historians
	- Memory is highly unreliable - interviewees may struggle to remember events or may misremember important information
