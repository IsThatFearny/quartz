# Early Modern Tutorial 3

1. Quiccheberg offers a set of labels for how to organise your own collection—skim these, before reading pp. 73-77 more closely. How does he expect his audience of merchants, rich artisans, or nobles to use it, judging from his ‘Recommendations and Advice’?
2. Compare Ole Worm’s frontispiece (which comes at the beginning of a description of his own collection). Quiccheberg and Worm both hint at travel: how does the collection say something about knowledge of the world, and about the possessor of such a collection?
3. On pp. 71-73, Quiccheberg describes some workshops as part of his ideal Theatrum, reflecting a growing early modern interest in craft and how craftspeople knew the world (see Findlen), often seen as early version of scientific laboratories. Consider either the counterfieting of coral, or the material ‘Dragon’s blood’, a plant substance used in medicines, makeups, and as a dye. Why might these craft products be interesting in a _Wunderkammer_? Is there a clear line between the ‘natural’ and ‘artificial’? We do not know the name of the author-practitioner who wrote _BnF Ms. Fr. 640_, but we do know the name of Quiccheberg: what does that fact say about the social organisation of knowledge?
