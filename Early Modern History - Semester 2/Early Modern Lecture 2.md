### Printing

#### Gutenberg's invention

The invention of the printing press by Johannes Gutenberg (as the bible) was often regarded as a discovery on par with the invention of gunpowder and the discovery of the Americas.

In the early 15th century, the demand for books and manuscripts surged. These were originally handwritten on vellum (although later paper became more widely used), usually by monks (who were one of the few literate groups), and as such took a very long time to write and subsequently replicate. With the advent of the printing press, books could be replicated significantly faster, and the process made cheaper, bringing down the sale price of books on the whole. 

Paper was not the only thing that could be used in the printing press - wood blocks and metal could be printed upon as well, allowing the printing of images such as maps.

</br>

#### The Global History of Printing

Although Gutenberg brought printed books to Europe, printing had been invented by those in Asia for a very long time before hand. However, this was largely performed by individuals (e.g. families, governments, etc.), and not by businessmen interested in the sale of books. Likewise, the complicated nature of many Asian scripts, with thousands of characters (as opposed to the Latin alphabet's 10s), prevented movable type from being implemented widely in Asia.

</br>

#### The growth of printing

- By 1500, 250 cities in Europe had printing presses (e.g. Lisbon, London, Stockholm, etc.)
- By 1600, 350,000 different publications had been printed, totally roughly one million individual copies
- Printed texts were estimated to be 80% cheaper than their handwritten equivalents
- Historians estimate that literacy increased by 10-15% during this period
