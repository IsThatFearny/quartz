### The Renaissance

- 'Rebirth' of classical learning/ancient knowledge
- Initially began in c. 14th-c. 15th Italy
- Largely using Greek and Latin texts
- 'Ad Fontes' - going back to the sources
- A large focus on historical self-awareness

**N.B.** It is important to note that the use of the term 'humanism' in this context does not equate to the modern conception of the word, denoting secularism and atheism; rather, renaissance humanism was the movement that argued for the study of classical texts

</br>

### Renaissance Monarchy

- Scottish renaissance monarchs were James III (1460-1488); James IV (1488-1513); and James V (1513-42)
- A key symbol of renaissance monarchy is the imperial crown (closed at the top, as seen in the steeple of St Giles). These were originally only worn by Holy Roman Emperors, but renaissance mon archs began to wear them. The crown symbolises 'full jurisdiction and free empire within his realm' (the words of the Scottish parliament in relation to James III)
- In the late 15th-16th century, there was a major change in architecture of royal palaces - they became more grandiose, hiring foreign architects and developing a new architectural style

</br>

### Renaissance literature

- Renaissance literature had a moral and philosophical purpose, generally using the Latin style characteristic of Petrarch; Cicero and Virgil
- Literature focusing on history began to be developed (e.g. the *Scotorum Historia* of Hector Boece in 1527)
- Rediscovered texts began to be translated into other languages (e.g. Scots and English)
