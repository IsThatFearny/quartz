## The Professionalisation of History as a Discipline


### Leopold von Ranke (1795-1886)

- 1814: Studied Classics and Theology at the University of Leipzig, and trained in philology
- 1824: Wrote *History of the Latin and Teutonic Peoples from 1491-1514*
- 1825: Achieves a position at the University of Berlin
- 1871: Retires, leaving a corpus of over 50 volumes
- 1874: Becomes the Prussian Privy Councillor
- 1884: Becomes the first honorary member of the American Historical Association

</br>

#### Ethos

Ranke's ethos was empiricism, i.e. the belief that history should be entirely based on empirical data without bias:


> [!quote] Leopold von Ranke, in the preface to the *History of the Latin and Teutonic Peoples*
> "To history has been given the function of judging the past, of instructing men [sic] for the profit of future years. The present attempt does not aspire to such lofty undertaking. It merely wants to show the past as it essentially was (wie es eigentlich gewesen). …Strict presentation of facts, no matter how conditional and attractive they might be, is undoubtedly the supreme law."


</br>

#### Archives

Ranke's opinion was that such empiricism should be based primarily on archival work, using critical study of physical documents in order to uncover information. Ranke said this included memoirs, diaries, letters, reports from embassies, and eyewitness accounts. This study was enabled by a boom in the critical publishing of documents, in particular the production of 'critical editions'. These editions collated various other editions of the works, and would establish the authentic and original text of the work, allowing researches and archivists to easily access these documents. Critical editions did not come about in the 19th century as there were various editions based on theology and classical texts, but instead the 19th century saw greater production of critical editions based on historical and political works.

</br>

#### Institutionalisation

The model of the university that was globalised most successfully was the one that emerged in the early 19th century in what is now Germany. These universities connected research and teaching (those that teach also research and practise the subject), gave formal training in research (a doctorate), and utilised a seminar system, not just lectures with no student participation. Alongside institutionalisation came the formation of new textual communities, e.g. scholarly historical journals. 

</br>

#### Problems

The emphasis on archives and research based on said archives led to a narrowing of history during the 19th century inadvertently led to the erasure of certain history, particularly those which could not be written down (i.e. oral histories) or those parts of history which were not deemed worthy of being included in the archive. This meant that certain perspectives were highly privileged above others.

Likewise, there is also another side to institutionalisation, with the main issue being that certain groups were excluded from academia and professionalisation. For example, women and minorities were unable to go to many universities where history became professionalised, and even if they were, their career progression would not be on the same level as their male counterparts.
