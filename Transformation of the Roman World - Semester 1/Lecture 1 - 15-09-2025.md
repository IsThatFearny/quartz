## c. 300-800 Overview - Towards Byzantium and the Early Medieval West

- Split into 4 Emperors (known as the Tetrachy)
- The Tetrachy did not last long - after Diocletian retired, Constantine entered Rome and had the senate declare him the senior emperor of the empire
- Constantine was the first emperor to identify closely with the Judeo-Christian concept of 'god'

## Constantine and Constantinople

- Founded on the site of Byzantium in 324
- Characterised as the Second/'new' Rome, and named after Constantine
- The empire was divided in 395 into East and West
- Rome remained the seat of absolute authority - Constantinople was simply another 'capital' (although this term is somewhat anachronistic)
- There was a linguistic divide across the empire - Greek vs Latin as the language of the elites

</br>

## The 'Fall' of the Western Empire - A timeline
- **476 AD** - Western Emperor Romulus is not replaced and retires in Naples, as barbarian generals take control of the remnants of the Roman Empire
- Theoderic became the 'King of Italy', and still appointed senators as Consuls, held gladiatorial games, etc.
- **535 AD** - Justinian begins the reconquest of Italy; he is a native speaker of Latin and reclaims North Africa and Rome
- **Early 7th Century** - The Last Great War of Antiquity involved a coup and a replacement of the emperor. The war involved Rome against Persia, and the war ends when the Persians launch a coup and replace their head of state
- **632 AD** - Death of Muhammad, followed by multiple Arab invasions
- **800 AD** - Empress Irene was ruling, although Charlemagne was crowned as 'Holy Roman Emperor' by the Pope in Rome due to disbelief in female authority

</br>

## Historiography

- Some scholars do not accept the 'Decline and Fall' (as coined by Edward Gibbon, who has a longer late antiquity than most of his fellows) - they prefer to characterise the late Roman Empire as a transformation rather than a 'fall'
- People identified as Romans long after the 'end' of the empire

</br>

## Byzantium

- Byzantium was a Greek city which Constantinople was a replacement for, whose people were called 'Byzantoi'
- Its modern use is to disassociate the Medieval Empire from the Roman Empire
- The term is generally problematic, but is used so regularly because it is easy and clear to understand
- It also has issues with periodisation, as Archaeologists use 'Byzantine' to describe periods which Historians tend to refer to as 'late Roman'
