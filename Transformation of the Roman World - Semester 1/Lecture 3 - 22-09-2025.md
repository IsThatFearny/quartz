### Constantine's Legacy

**Key achievements**
- Restructuring of the Roman army
- Reform of the coinage system
- A new rulership ideology
- Founded the new imperial city of Constantinople
- First emperor to show imperial support for Christianity

</br>

### The collapse of the tetrarchy and the rise of Constantine

**Timeline:**

- 305 CE: Diocletian and Maximian retire; Galerius and Constantius become Augusti; Maximinus Daza and Severus appointed Caesars
- 306 CE: Constantius dies in York (25 July); Constantine assumes the title of Augustus, as does Maxentius; Galerius recognises Constantine as Caesar and sends Severus against Maxentius; Severus' army defects and Severus is killed
- 307 CE: Maximian returns from retirement, following son (Maxentius)’s rebellion Alliance proposed between Constantine and Maximian & Maxentius Maximian acknowledges/promotes Constantine as Augustus, and marries his daughter Fausta to him

</br>

Constantine began a new style of imperial self-representation, depicting himself as younger than he actually was (i.e. beardless) on coinage, as well as using symbols of his victories on coinage as well to demonstrate his power and achievements.

</br>

### The Two Visions of Constantine

Constantine's alleged visions exploited patterns of communication with the gods as repeated throughout Roman history:

- A 'wonder' is witnessed
- A moment of amazement and uncertainty follows
- A person endowed with felicitas receives the correct interpretation from the gods themselves

Thus, in Constantine's visions, Christ behaves exactly like all other Roman (and Pagan) gods
