# Long Essay

**How have modern attitudes to empire affected the study of imperialism in the**  
**ancient world? Is it right that they should do so?**

---

The study of imperialism in the ancient world (largely that of the Greeks and Romans) often suffers from anachronistic scholarship, imposing modern views (largely formed from our modern conception of 'imperialism') onto societies which had no such concepts. Modern attitudes towards empire include the introduction of 'colonialism' as a lens through which to study imperialism, and the notion of empire as a mission with the aim of dominion over another population. Neither of these modern attitudes accurately reflect imperialism in the ancient world, and thus this essay shall argue that, as a result of said inaccuracy, it is not right that contemporary academia should treat ancient empire as one and the same as the imperialism in more recent years (such as that of the British Empire).

To analyse how contemporary attitudes towards empire negatively affect the study of ancient imperialism, one must first note the major differences between the ancient world and more recent empires. One of the most important distinctions is the development of the modern nation-state. Imperialism in the Peloponnese and the wider Greek world was not based on the dominance of one particular 'state', but rather operated through the *polis*, 'city-states' which had their own laws and customs.
