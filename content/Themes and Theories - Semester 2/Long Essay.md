# Long Essay

</br>

**How have modern attitudes to empire affected the study of imperialism in the**  
**ancient world? Is it right that they should do so?**

---

The study of imperialism in the ancient world (largely that of the Greeks and Romans) often suffers from anachronistic scholarship, imposing modern views (largely formed from our modern conception of 'imperialism') onto societies which had no such concepts. Modern attitudes towards empire include the introduction of 'colonialism' as a lens through which to study imperialism, and the notion of empire as a mission with the aim of dominion over another population. Neither of these modern attitudes accurately reflect imperialism in the ancient world, and thus this essay shall argue that, as a result of said inaccuracy, it is not right that contemporary academia should treat ancient empire as one and the same as the imperialism in more recent years (such as that of the British Empire).

To analyse how contemporary attitudes towards empire negatively affect the study of ancient imperialism, one must first note the major differences between the ancient world and more recent empires. One of the most important distinctions is the development of the modern nation-state. Imperialism in the Peloponnese and the wider Greek world was not based on the dominance of one particular 'state', but rather operated through the *polis*, 'city-states' which had their own laws and customs. These poleis, whilst often under Spartan or Athenian hegemony, did not answer directly to a centralised governmental structure as has been the case with more modern empires, but instead were often subject to a form of imperialism by consent, with a recognition that this would be the best arrangement if the *polis* were to remain financially and militarily efficient. The territories of the Roman Empire, whilst adhering to a much stricter form of centralised government with power vested in the Emperor, still did not fall under the modern definition of 'nation-states', as much of them were made up of autonomous townships (albeit with varying degrees of independence from Roman rule). Modern scholars of empire often define imperialism as the dominance of one nation-state (such as Britain) over another (such as India), a model which clearly does not fit with the forms which ancient empires took.
