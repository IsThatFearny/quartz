The three given translations (AI-Generated, Edwards, and Hanford) each interpret the original Latin in their own different ways. Whilst often the 'gist' of the text remains, each has strengths and weaknesses in terms of vocabulary choice, fluency in English, interpretation of word order, etc. This essay shall identify these differences and and provide a line by line comparison between each text.

In the opening few lines of the passage, many differences are plain to see. The AI and the Edwards translations treat '*omnis*' differently: the AI treats it as an adjective, meaning 'all'; Edwards translates it as a noun, meaning 'a whole'. It is, however, an adjective. Hanford entirely omits '*divisa*', instead choosing to use 'comprises'. 'Divided' and 'comprises' can, in context, have completely different meanings, and so the more literal translation may be better here. '*incolunt*' is in the active voice - the Belgae actively inhabit the territory, and this is picked up on by the Ai Translation. Edwards and Hanford, by contrast, choose to translate it as being in the passive voice - the territory is inhabited *by* the Belgae (this analysis also appli) If this were the case, then one might expect to see '*incoluntur*'. Hanford also omits '*unam*' and includes 'respectively', which does not appear in the original Latin.
