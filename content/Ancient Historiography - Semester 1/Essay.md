## What can we say about the audience of Josephus and how did this shape his approach to history?

</br>

Flavius Josephus (c. 37-100 CE) is often characterised as a 'Judeo-Roman' historian, positioned in between Roman, Greek, and Judean literary and historiographical traditions. His audience was, in a similar fashion, comprised of multiple different ethnic, political, and social groups, and his writing often reflects such a diverse readership. In writing *Bellum Judaicum*, *Antiquitates Judicae*, *Contra Apionem* and *Vita*, Josephus charted a course intersecting the views of his readership so as not to offend their sensibilities or to bring himself into disrepute. This essay shall discuss the composition of Josephus' audience, and analyse the content (and lack thereof) of his works, his use of rhetoric, and his presentation of controversial events to conclude that Josephus' audience caused his methodology in writing history to tread the line between apologetic retelling and accurate reporting.
