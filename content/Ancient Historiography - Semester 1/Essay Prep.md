## What can we say about the audience of Josephus and how did this shape his approach to history?

</br>

I. Introduction Briefly introduce Josephus, his works (e.g., The Jewish War, Antiquities of the Jews), and the historical context in which he wrote. Explain the importance of understanding his audience and how it influenced his historiography.

II. Identifying Josephus' audience Discuss the potential audiences for Josephus' works, including: * Jewish readers in Judea and the diaspora * Roman readers, potentially including the imperial family and aristocracy * Greek readers, who might be interested in Jewish history and culture * Christian readers, who might be familiar with Josephus' works through early Christian writers

III. The impact of audience on Josephus' approach to history Explore how Josephus' perceived audience shaped his historical narrative, including: * Apologetic tendencies: How did Josephus present Jewish history and culture to-counter potential criticisms or misconceptions from non-Jewish readers? * Selectivity and omission: What events, figures, or themes did Josephus emphasize or downplay to suit his audience's interests and biases? * Rhetorical strategies: How did Josephus use rhetorical devices, such as pathos, ethos, and logos, to persuade and engage his readers?

IV. Key themes and examples Delve deeper into specific themes and examples that illustrate the influence of Josephus' audience on his historiography, such as: * The portrayal of the Jewish revolt against Rome (66-73 CE): How did Josephus present the revolt to his Roman and Jewish readers? * The representation of Jewish leaders and figures: How did Josephus depict key figures like Herod the Great, the Hasmoneans, or the prophets to appeal to different audience segments? * The use of biblical and extra-biblical sources: How did Josephus incorporate and interpret these sources to create a narrative that resonated with his audience?

V. Conclusion Summarize the main points and reiterate how understanding Josephus' audience is essential to appreciating his historical approach. Reflect on the implications of this analysis for our broader understanding of ancient historiography and the complexities of writing history for diverse audiences.
