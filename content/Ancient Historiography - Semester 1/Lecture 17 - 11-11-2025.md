## Appian


### Biography

- Born c. 95 in Alexandria
- Held public office
- Lived through Jewish Revolt (115-117)
- Alexandrian Greek; also Roman citizen
- Moved to Rome in c. 120 to pursue career as a lawyer - can do this because of his roman citizenship
- Imperial Procurator (unsure of what, but probably financial management) under Antoninus Pius (138-161)
- Tutored Marcus Aurelius
- Wrote *Roman History* in the 150s
- died c. 165

</br>

### Appian's work

- Autobiography - now lost
- *Roman History*
	- Written in Greek; heavily condensed
	- 24 books arranged geographically "nation by nation"
	- Less centred on Rome and told from the periphery
	- History of Rome's subjection of people's
	- The one **exception** is the 5 books (13-17) on the civil wars (divided according to the military leaders of the factions)

</br>

### Ethnographic approach


> [!quote] Appian, *Roman History*, Preface 12
> "AS I begin the process of writing up my Roman history, I thought I should first set out the boundaries of all the nations under Roman rule. They are as follows: *proceeds to list all Rome's contemporary possessions and frontiers*"

</br>

### Appian's Process

- Travelled throughout the empire ("from Carthage to Iberia... to Sicily or Macedonia")
- "wanted to find out the relationship of Rome which each of [these territories] in order to establish the weakness or endurance of the nations"
- "Rome's dealings with other states that took place at the same time, I am extracting and trasnferring to the relevant place"
- Regarded it "excessive" to date every event, so reserved dating for important occasions
