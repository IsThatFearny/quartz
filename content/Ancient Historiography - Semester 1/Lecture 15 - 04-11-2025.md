## Tacitus and Imperial History

- "In History, we hold our own with the Greeks" - Rome and Greece are on equal footing with Greek historians - Tacitus makes equations between Livy and Herodotus, and Sallust and Thucydides
- Also mentions various authors and historians whose works no longer survive - shows that Tacitus was writing amongst many more authors than one might think from the sources (e.g. Pliny the Elder, Cremutius Crodus, Aufidius Bassus, etc.)

</br>

**Is there something that changes if there is a revolution within the state?**

- War and politics usually the focus of historiography
- Tacitus - "the state is now Caesar"

### Velleius Paterculus

- Another surviving source
- Wrote under Tiberius (second Roman emperor)
- Writes of Tiberius' 'superhuman achievements' - a severe contrast to the way that Tiberius is described in Tacitus' *Annals*, in which he is portrayed as a tyrant
- first book missing first few pages (plus title and dedication)  
	- from just before the fall of Troy  
	- early history of Greece (in seven chapters)  
	- founding of Rome (chapter 8)  
	- large lacuna (from reign of Romulus to battle of Pydna in 168 BC)  
	- ends with the fall of Carthage (146)
