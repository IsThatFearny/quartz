## Prep

- What signs of barbarity does Pigafetta detect in the islanders? In what ways does their representation serve to highlight superior European civilization?

Pigafetta often refers to the indigenous peoples that he comes across as 'going naked like the others', also giving reference to their referral to him and his crew as 'holy bodies'.



- The Cantino planisphere is a manuscript, designed around ‘rumb lines’; the Waldseemuller world map is a printed version, perhaps originally made in 1000 copies, and designed to cover a very large wall. What different purposes might these two media serve?
- What does the Cantino planisphere’s history, originally smuggled out of Lisbon, suggest about the purpose of this kind of knowledge? Looking at the map, why might a ruler or merchant or visiting noble find this useful and interesting?
- The Waldseemuller world map appears as a mathematical grid, but also incorporates many visual. Single out two or of these and consider what purposes these might have served. Why would one possess this kind of map? Why does Amerigo Vespucci appear with the ancient authority Ptolemy at the top of Martin Waldseemüller’s world map of 1507?
- The Treaty of Tordesillas (1494)—represented by a vertical line running north to south, through Brazil, which reflects a deep uncertainty about where one ocean becomes another. Portugal wanted Brazil, with its legendary commodities (also represented on the Cantino planisphere). The treaty granted Portugal this part of Brazil; Ferdinand and Isabella took the west for Spain. Consider what this implies also for the other oceans. Since the treaty only specified one border, who ‘owned’ the Pacific?
- Matteo Ricci was a Jesuit who arrived in China in 1583, and established a mission there, who established relationships with the imperial court. This is a wood block print, possibly devised in collaboration with the mathematician Li Zhizao. What strikes you most about the positioning of the oceans?
