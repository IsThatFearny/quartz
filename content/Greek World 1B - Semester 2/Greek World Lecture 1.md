### Origins of the Athenian Empire

- Athenians rebuilt the city and its walls (in order to keep the 'barbarians' which had plagued them so recently out)
- Themistocles encouraged the building of the Peiraeus, in order to develop Athens into a naval power (this would be key in future conquests against the Persians and otherwise)
- Spartan withdrawal of leadership of the Greek powers against the Persians allowed Athens to take over the hegemony, leading the alliance; Athens had control over which cities should provide money, which should provide ships, etc.
- The Athenians instituted the office of 'Treasures to the Greeks' (*Hellenotamiai*), which was responsible for receiving the tribute (*phoros*). The treasury was on the island of Delos, and this was where the allied powers met
- Aristides, son of Lysimachus, prompted the Ionian's defection from the Spartan alliance
- In the intervening period between the Persian War and the Peloponnesian War, Athens went from leading autonomous allies with common policy, to having significant control over politics, management of war, and finances
- Delian league established in 478 BC, with (allegedly) anti-Persian purposes

### Key Dates/Simplified Chronology

- Foundation of Delian League: Themistocles and Aristides (478 BC)  
- First campaigns: Cimon (470s–460s BC)  
- Consolidation: Pericles (461–429 BC)  
- Mature phase: Cleon (420s BC)  
- Dissolution: defeat of Athens in Peloponnesian War (404 BC)

### Functionality

#### The Tribute
- Tribute introduced at the foundation of the Delian league (originally 460 talents)
- From c. 454 BC (when the treasury was moved from Delos to Athens), we have tribute lists that record the amounts given
- The tribute lists are a record of the *aparchai* (first-fruits), i.e. one-sixtieth of the tribute, given as an offering to Athena (thus if using for comparison, multiply by sixty)
- 330 allies recorded in the tribute lists
- Assessments of tributes were usually revised every four years
- Tribute was replaced by a 5% import/export tax in 413 BC
- The treasury employed 'tax-collectors' to exact the tribute from those cities which did not pay/fell short of the required amount

#### Relations with communities

- Athens was not very harsh in their relations with other communities - e.g. the Phaselites were offered a favourable method of legal proceedings/prosecution
- However, some regulations were harsher - the oath taken by councillors (elected government representatives) required them to swear to obey Athens and her allies
