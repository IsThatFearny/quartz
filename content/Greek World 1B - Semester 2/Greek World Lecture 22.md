# Greek World Lecture 22

</br>

- 'Hellenistic' was originally a characteristic which meant that a community lived in a city or urban area, rather than rurally and without a civic centre
- The polis was seen as a sort of partnership which aimed at the good of the general community (according to Aristotle)
- Aristotle argued that the polis was not simply a Greek phenomenon - Carthage, Sparta, and Crete are said to have 'good' constitutions which aim at the good of the community
- Citizens were subdivided, raised in a deme as members if a certain tribe. Citizens were then able to qualify for training at the Gymnasium, and were then eligible for legitimate marriage, priesthoods, and magistracies, and were able to vote in the council and at the assembly
