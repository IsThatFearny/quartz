The Peloponnesian war tends to be divided into three separate wars:

- The "Ten-year" or "Archidamian" War (431-421)
- Melos (416) and the Sicilian expedition (415-413)
- The "Decelean" War (413-404)

This periodisation is taken from the works of Thucydides - he separates his history into these three sections (although does acknowledge that he is writing about the war as a whole)

</br>

### The Method of Thucydides

- He confesses that it is 'hard to reproduce the exact words used' in various speeches, and therefore makes 'each speaker say broadly what [he] supposed would have been needed'
- Thucydides was able to offer another perspective, as he was exiled. He could therefore provide an account of the Peloponnesian side of the conflict
- His history was intended as a 'permanent legacy' rather than a 'showpiece for a singular hearing'
- The work is set out in chronological order, subdividing by phases and summers/winters (he asserts that measuring by the terms of various office-holders is an inaccurate method)

</br>

### Causes of the War

- A civil war broke out in Epidamnos (a colony of Corcyra) in 435 BC
	- Oligarchs sought help from Corcyra
	- Democrats sought help from the motherland, Corinth
- This turned into a conflict between Corcyra and Corinth - Corinth sought help from Sparta; and Corcyra sought an Athenian alliance
- Corinth perceived Athenian intervention as an intrusion into their affairs

</br>

- In 433 BC, Athens demanded that Potidaea (a colony of Corinth and an ally of Athens), tear down its walls
- Potidaea revolted in protest, with support from both Sparta and Corinth
- Athens therefore beseiged Potidaea

</br>

- Athens imposed an embargo on Megara (a Spartan ally who had supported Corinth during the Corinthian Civil War)
- Megarians were banned from Athenian-controlled ports
- Sparta demanded that Athens revoke their decree, and Athens refused

</br>

Note here the heavy involvement of Corinth - there was significant Corinthian influence in the immediate causes of the war: they asked Sparta and the Peloponnesian League to intervene in their conflicts against Athens. 

</br>

### The Demands of the Spartans

- Expulsion of the descendants of those responsible for the murder of the followers of Kylon (the leader of a coup in the late 7th century)
- Lift the siege of Potidaea
- Return autonomy to the Auginetans
- Recall the decree that excluded the Megarians from Athenian markets and harbours

**The Spartans asserted that there would be no war if the Athenians restored autonomy to the Greeks**

</br>

### The Speeches of Pericles

Pericles made several speeches (although the funeral oration is by far the most famous): 1) The call to war (432 BC); 2) The speech on war strategy (431 BC - Thucydides only summarises this); 3) The funeral oration (431/0 BC); and 4) The speech on the continuation of the war (430 BC).

#### The Contents of the First Speech

- Pericles insisted that Athens should not concede to Sparta's demands, or else they would ask for more
- He said that war is inevitable, and thus it is better to fight on their own terms rather than those of their enemies
- Encouraged confidence about the strengths of Athens (i.e. financial and naval superiority)
- Insistence that Sparta could not win, as they relied on land power and lacked resources to sustain the war - Athens would use their naval power to defeat the Spartans

</br>

#### The Second Speech

- Athenians buried the dead of the first year of the war at public expense - this was in accordance with ancient customs
- It was customary for an esteemed man and skilled orator to deliver a commemorative address
- The speech was not only a funeral oration, but also praised Athenian democracy and Athenian values:
	- Merit, rather than class, determined advancement
	- Athenians valued individuality, able to live freely in both private and public life
	- Athenians balanced their private affairs with public duties
	- Despite a relaxed lifestyle, Athens was able to excel in war
	- **Athenian superiority made all sacrifices worthwhile**

</br>

### The Third Speech

- Made in the second year of the war
- The situation was unfavourable for Athens - Attica had invaded twice, and there was a plague outbreak
- Athenians were angry, accusing Pericles of having led them into futile war
- Athens attempted negotiations with the Spartans, but had no success
- Pericles convened an extraordinary assembly, issuing an apology to the Athenians
- Pericles stressed that Athens could not afford to release the empire for fear of repercussions

</br>

### Later Chronology

- 428: Revolt of Mytilene  
- 425: Battles of Pylos and Sphacteria  
- 424–422: The war moves to the North: Brasidas’ campaign to Thrace and Macedonia. Capture of Amphipolis.  
- 422: (Second) battle of Amphipolis. Death of Brasidas and Cleon.  
- 421: Peace of Nicias  

**The protagonist throughout these events was Cleon (until his death in 422)**
