The Peloponnesian war tends to be divided into three separate wars:

- The "Ten-year" or "Archidamian" War (431-421)
- Melos (416) and the Sicilian expedition (415-413)
- The "Decelean" War (413-404)

This periodisation is taken from the works of Thucydides - he separates his history into these three sections (although does acknowledge that he is writing about the war as a whole)

</br>

### The Method of Thucydides

- He confesses that it is 'hard to reproduce the exact words used' in various speeches, and therefore makes 'each speaker say broadly what [he] supposed would have been needed'
- Thucydides was able to offer another perspective, as he was exiled. He could therefore provide an account of the Peloponnesian side of the conflict
- His history was intended as a 'permanent legacy' rather than a 'showpiece for a singular hearing'
- The work is set out in chronological order, subdividing by phases and summers/winters (he asserts that measuring by the terms of various office-holders is an inaccurate method)

</br>

### Causes of the War

- A civil war broke out in Epidamnos (a colony of Corcyra) in 435 BC
	- Oligarchs sought help from Corcyra
	- Democrats sought help from the motherland, Corinth
- This turned into a conflict between Corcyra and Corinth - Corinth sought help from Sparta; and Corcyra sought an Athenian alliance
- Corinth perceived Athenian intervention as an intrusion into their affairs
