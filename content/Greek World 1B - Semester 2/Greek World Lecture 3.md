#### The Parthenon

- Dates to c. 447-432BC
- Presumed to be a temple to Athena (manifestation named *Athena Parthenos*, hence the name)
	- N.B. presumed as we have no evidence of dedications to Athena Parthenos, nor do we know the location of her altar
- Designed by Iktinos and Kallikrates; the chief sculptor was Pheidias (with many other sculptors working below him)
- Built with Pentelic marble (local Athenian marble)
- 'Peristyle' columns, making the Parthenon a 'Peripteral building'
- One would traditionally enter the Parthenon from the East
- Larger room was called the *cella*; the other room, separated entirely from it, named the *adyton*.
- The East entrance (porch) was named the *opisthodomos*; the West entrance was named the *pronaos*
- Number of columns on the long side are double +1 the number on the short side - this was considered the 'ideal' number
- A combination of doric (in all columns but the four in the adyton) and ionic (the four columns in the Adyton, which have a base - although there is debate over whether they are ionic or corinthian) architecture, unlike the vast majority of contemporary Greek temples
- Multiple temples were built on the site before Parthenon - it is the third iteration of the temple (see below)

- No straight lines used in the Parthenon - this is because it is built so that the floor curves upwards towards the centre, so that the highest point is in this spot (the same is true for the roof)
- All columns incline slightly inwards - at the corners they incline inwards *diagonally*.
