### Pylos and Sphacteria (425)

Demosthenes (an Athenian general) was driven to Pylos whilst sailing along the Peloponnese. The Athenians built a fortified camp on the island, which Sparta responded to by sending their forces from the island of Sphacteria. Athens captures Spartan soldiers on Sphacteria, with Cleon taking command.

</br>

### Moving northwards

The Spartan general Brasidas led an expedition to Thrace and Macedonia with the aim of weakening Athenian strongholds in the north. He took the Athenian colony of Amphipolis (Thucydides was called to defend it but arrived too late, and was exiled afterwards). Cleon was sent in 422 to retake the city (second battle of Amphopolis). **Both Cleon and Brasidas were killed in the battle**, and as such both sides are willing to negotiate peace (the peace of Nicias in 421)

</br>

### Stipulations of the peace of Nicias

- A fifty-year truce
- All captured territories to be returned to their original occupier
- All prisoners on both sides to be released to their home city
- All hostilities towards one another to be ceased in their entirety
- All future disputes to be resolved through diplomatic and judicial means, rather than war

</br>

The peace held for **ten years**, but after this period hostilities continued:

</br>

### War after the 'peace' timeline

- Athens sent an expedition to Melos in **416**
	- The Melians were strongly opposed to Athenian occupation, and were openly hostile towards Athens
	- Upon surrender, all Melian men were executed and all women and children enslaved
- An Athenian expedition travelled to Sicily from **415-413**
	- The Athenians underestimated the size of the force on the island (both Greek and 'barbarian'), and the physical size of the island itself - the war was much larger than expected (Thucydides likens it to the Peloponnesian war itself)
- The Ionian War took place from **413-404**
	- The Spartans invaded Attica and fortified Decelea, disrupting the grain supply to Athens
	- The commissioning of a Spartan naval fleet further disrupted grain supply by sea
	- Sparta signed a treaty with Persia, receiving financial aid towards the fleet
- In **411** the Oligarchy of the four hundred (Athenian coup) took place
	- **In Athens:** Peisander led a coup in Athens, establishing an oligarchic government - this new regime sought to end the war with Sparta through negotiation with Persia. The rule of the four hundred was characterised by violence and assassination. The dilution of the oligarchy to five thousand was promised, yet never delivered upon
	- **In Samos:** Athenian forces on board the trireme *Paralos*, stationed at Samos, became involved with a local civil conflict between oligarchs and democrats (the Athenians naturally supported the democratic faction). They received the news of the coup, and decided to restore democracy, recalling Alcibiades in the hope that he could gain the support of the Persians
	- **Back in Athens:** The Spartans exploited the chaos, attacking Euboi and destroying an Athenian fleet - this news led to the overthrow of the four hundred. The new oligarchy of the five thousand was established, although this was short lived. Democracy was restored after the naval victory at the Battle of Kyzikos in 410
- **404:** The fall of Athens and the introduction of the Thirty Tyrants

</br>

### The Siege of Syracuse

The Athenians made an attempt to encircle Syracuse entirely by building walls; however, the Syracusans received Spartan support, and when it came to a battle (in the middle of the night), it ended in complete disaster for the Athenians. The two generals, Nicias and Demosthenes, cannot agree on a plan, and this delay gave an advantage to the Spartans. The Spartans dominated by both land and sea, massacring 40,000 Athenians who attempted to withdraw by land. 7,000 survivors were imprisoned in open-air quarries

</br>

### From Thucydides to Xenephon

Thucydides' narrative stops just after the establishment of the oligarchic government of the five thousand. Xenophon's *Hellenica* picks it up, covering 411-362 in seven books. The end of the war is covered in books 1 and 2. It is theorised that this is the publishing of Thucydides unfinished work, with Xenophon taking credit (Book 1 begins with 'after this', and the prose style changes in book 3)

</br>

### The Final Phase

- Despite Athenian victory at the Battle of Arginusae in 406, commanders were executed for failing to rescue drowning sailors
- Spartan command is taken by Lysander, drawing on Persian finances to refurbish the fleet. He plans to attack Athenian strongholds in the Hellespont in order to cut off supply lines.
- Lysander set up camp at Abydos, with the Athenians responding by setting up camp on the opposite side of the strait, at Sestos
- The Battle of Aegospotamoi in 405 was a decisive Spartan victory
- The Athenians were forced to negotiate surrender. Athens' walls are torn down
