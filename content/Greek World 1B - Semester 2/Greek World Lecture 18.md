### Succession

- There were various centres of power throughout Alexander's empire: Pella in Macedonia itself of course, Babylon (where Alexander died), and the parts of Turkey in which Katelos and Antigonus were based
- Alexander had a half-brother (dubbed a 'half wit' by contemporary sources) and an unborn son. The ruling in terms of succession was that both were to be given power on the condition that he share power with the son when he was born

</br>

### The fight over Alexander's body

Alexander's body was bound for Aegae, the historical resting place of Macedonian kings. The procession, however, was stopped by Ptolemy of Egypt, who stole the carriage from its guards and took it back to Egypt. As a consequence, Perdiccas wages war on Egypt, although his campaign failed disastrously, resulting in his subsequent assassination.

</br>

### Guardians of the Kings

Arrhidaeus (Alexander's half-brother) were deemed 'the guardians of the kings', although they resigned the guardianship when it was discovered that Eurydice turning Macedonians against them. The same thing happened to their successor, Antipater, although he frightened Eurydice in his speech to a crowd and thus persuaded her to cease her efforts

</br>

### Olympias' control

Olympias (the grandmother of Alexander's child) captured the royal family and seized the kingdom bloodlessly. She placed Eurydice and her husband Philip under guard and mistreated them, eventually ordering some Thracians to stab Philip to death.

</br>

### The Treaty

A treaty was decided upon to end the succession battle. Cassander, Ptolemy, and Lysimachus agreed with Antigonus that Cassander would be 'general of Europe' until Alexander's son came of age; that Lysimachus would rule Thrace; and that Ptolemy should rule Egypt and Arabia. Antigonus was take precedence in Asia, and the Greeks would be autonomous.
