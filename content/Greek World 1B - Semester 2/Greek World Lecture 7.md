### Programme

- The 'proagon': poets, actors, and chorus speak about their productions
- A figure of Dionysus is dressed, garlanded, and paraded in procession from the temple next to the Acropolis to a sanctuary outside the city. Sacrifices would be made, and the figure paraded by torchlight back into the city
- All public businesses was then suspended. Residents would parade carrying items for the feast (including the main bull). Hymns would be sung, and later in the day the Dithyramb competition would be held
- The dramatic competition would then be held

Prior to the play, there was a ritual:

1) The theatre would be 'purified'
2) The ten generals would give a libation
3) Public proclamations made
4) A public display of the tribute from allies
5) Orphaned sons of those who died in war awarded a suit of armour
6) Judges and the order of the competition decided by lot

### Dramaturgical Basics

- The actors and chorus were all in costumes and masks - 2 or 3 actors, 10-15 chorus members
- Spoken verse (usually in iambic trimester) was alternated with sung verse (the chorus might split up to sing different parts)
- Structure:
	- Prologue
	- *parados* (entrance of the chorus)
	- Act 1
	- Choral Ode 1 (*stasimon*)
	- Act 2
	- etc.
- Specific scene types, such as *agon* (contest of speeches); the speech of a messenger; *stichomythia* (alternation line-by-line); and off-stage violence
- No curtain; in most cases, no one was on stage throughout a choral ode
- The stage was elevated with a space for the chorus
- Stage building would have 1-3 doors, with scenes painted on the background
- A cart could be rolled out the door, and there was a crane (*machina*) to raise actors in order to portray them as gods.
- Characters could also act on the roof or in the orchestra

### Aeschylus

- Born 524 BCE, Died 456 BCE
- Won his first victory in 484 BCE
- Fought at Marathon and in the Persian Wars
- Wrote c. 90 plays (22 productions of 4 plays)
- We have 7 whole plays
