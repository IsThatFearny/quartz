### What was a slave?


> [!quote] Homer, *Odyssey* 17.320-324
> "Slaves, when their masters lose their power, are no longer minded thereafter to do honest service: for Zeus, whose voice is borne afar, takes away half his worth from a man, when the day of slavery comes upon him"

Slaves were considered to be 'half a man' from the moment of the enslavement, even if their master were to die or lose control over the them. They would have slave status permanently.

</br>

> [!quote] Aristotle, *Politics* 1253b
> "The manager of a household must have his tools, and of tools some are lifeless and others living...; so also an article of property is a tool for the purpose of life, and property generally is a collection of tools, and a slave is a live article of property"

Slaves were a 'living tool' which a master could use at their will, even if one was not owned by another. Slaves were dehumanised and thus it was socially acceptable to use them in this way.

</br>

### Who was a slave?

The basic definition of a slave which Greek authors would largely adhere to was either: those who become slaves by conquest (i.e. those enslaved after the territory in which they lived was captured by a hostile force); or those barbarians who were enslaved and subsequently sold to slave owners

There were also occasions of enslavement of regular citizens as punishment:


> [!quote] Strabo 6.3.2, quoting Antiochus of Syracuse
> "After the Messenian war broke out, those of the Lacedaemonians who did not take part in the expedition were adjudged slaves and were named Helots, and all children who were born in the time of the expedition were called "Children of Virgins" and judicially deprived of the rights of citizenship"

</br>

**How many slaves?**


> [!quote] Ktesikles BNJ 245 frg. 1
> "Ktesikles in the third book of his chronicles states that a census of the Attic population was conducted in Athens by Demetrios of Phaleron during the 11[...]th Olympiad, counting 21,000 Athenian citizens, 10,000 metics, and **400,000 slaves** 

This is an embellishment, but speaks to the large amount of slaves that were present in Athens compared to those with citizenship. In any case, we can be sure that there was a very large contingent of slaves in Greece. A better estimate may be around 50,000-100,000 in Athens alone, although of course there is still a sizeable margin of error.
