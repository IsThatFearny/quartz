### Geography

- Ancient Macedonia was close to modern day Macedonia, but slightly south-east of the modern borders (its capital was Pella, just west of Thessaloniki) 
- Ancient Macedonia was not entirely Greek - the Greeks were unsure quite where the state lay in terms of geopolitics
- The 'old' capital (before Pella) was Aigai, which was the capital of Macedonia from the 7th century to the 5th, when a transition to Pella was put in action
- The centre of Macedonia began as quite a small region, however they led various conquests throughout the 7th-5th centuries and greatly expanded their borders into the north-east of the Greek world

</br>

### The Royal Dynasty

- The royal lineage, Herodotus claims, derived from Greece (Perdiccas, the first king, allegedly came from Argos and was a descendant of Heracles)
- Perdiccas and his two brothers were banished from Argos and came to Macedonia
- Various different authors define different lineages pre-Perdiccas - Satyrus, Diodorus, and Justin all agree that the common ancestor of the line was a man called Caranus, although we know little about him - the genealogy tracing back to Heracles is widely agreed upon, however, by these authors and others in the future

</br>

### Macedonia in the Persian Wars

- Persia demanded the subjection of Greece and of Macedonia
- Herodotus writes of an embassy sent to Aigai to insist on submission, which resulted in the assassination of the entire embassy by Alexander's soldiers. This was kept from the Persians in order to avoid direct conflict, and the follow-up mission sent from Persia to find the fallen was given a bribe of a sum of money and Alexander's sister for their silence
- Alexander gave counsel to the Spartans, warning them against war with the Persians - the Spartans wholeheartedly rejected his advice on the basis that he was a 'tyrant' and a foreigner
- A reason, perhaps, for Greek sources not concluding whether Macedonia was Greek is due to their lack of participation in the alliance which fought the Persian war

</br>

### Macedonia in the Peloponnesian war

- Macedon had a role to play in causing the Pelopponesian war - Perdiccas wanted the revolt of Potidaea and the war, for he had his own designs on the region, it being directly on his borders. He encouraged the Athenians to fight the Spartan alliance
- Macedonia made a treaty with Athens, which controlled exports and military aid. The treaty also gives away that Macedonia controlled various fringe territories (which were relatively autonomous in their own right)
- The Macedonian kingdom seems to have been quite fragile, relying on the goodwill and allegiance of smaller regional powers for its size and strength
