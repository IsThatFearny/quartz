### The Pediments

- The pediment of the Parthenon (the end face of the roof structure) depicts a contest between Athena and Poseidon to decide who will be the patron deity of the city. Each presents a gift to the city in order to win the favour of the people of Athens. Poseidon throws his spear onto the acropolis, and creates a salt spring; Athena manifests an olive tree on the Acropolis. Athena is voted to be the winner. Surrounding this scene are legends of Athens' past
- Artemis is depicted delivering the news of the result of the contest, and is one of the best surviving depictions
- Artemis' clothing styling (wet drapery style, adhering against her body and thus both concealing and revealing the body) is characteristic of the late 430s
- Also depicted (on the opposite pediment) is the birth of Athena, who is born from the split in Zeus' skull caused by Hephaestus. This pediment was badly damaged in 1687 when a shell hit the Parthenon; and was also damaged when the building was turned into a church, requiring cutting into the side of the building, badly damaging the pediment
- Aphrodite is pictured on the bottom right of the latter pediment reclining onto the lap of another woman, with another sitting by. The identities of these other women is disputed. Aphrodite's 'wet drapery' style characterises her role as the goddess of sexual love/desire: her drapery adheres to her body much closer than the other two figures; at the groin, the drapery is wrapped around her legs, emphasising the groin area; and her abdomen is clearly visible. These features are meant to evoke a sort of 'sexual allure' surrounding Aphrodite

### Athena Parthenos Statue

- As one walked into the temple, there stood a statue of Athena Parthenos, made of a combination of ivory and gold (the body of ivory, the drapes of gold). She was 12.2m tall.

![[Pasted image 20250121163315.png|300]]

- Some of the gold came from the Delian league mines - some was melted down when financials were dire
- The exterior of the shield showed the battle against the amazons, the interior showed the battle against the giants; her sandals show the battle against the centaurs (these are also all shown on the metopese)
- The base of the statue shows the birth of Pandora (the first woman)

**N.B.** the statue does not survive, but there are copies (made by the Romans) on a much smaller scale that do survive by which we can guess at what the full size statue looked like (with the support of textual sources)

## Other buildings on the Acropolis
</br>

### The Propylaia

- Located on the west side of the Acropolis
- Used as a gateway to the Acropolis
- Constructed in c. 437-432 BC
- Stands on a predecessor of itself
- Combination of doric (across the front, at the back, and at the sides) and ionic columns (in the middle) - ionic columns do not taper as they go up (this is a characteristic of doric columns), and therefore were used so that the bases were not too big to allow more space, and to allow them to rise higher and still function as a supporting column
- Parts of the Proplyaia were built from Eleusinian limestone

</br>

### Temple of Athena Nike

- Located on the west side of the Acropolis (on the south-west of the Propylai)
- Constructed in c.427-424 BCE
- A purely ionic structure (as opposed to most other buildings on the Acropolis, which use a combination of doric and ionic architecture)
- 4 columns on each side of the building - tetrastyle amphiprostyle (i.e. four columns)
