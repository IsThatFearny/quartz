There were three main contenders for Greek hegemony (other smaller city-states also contested but they were not 'serious' opponents):

- Sparta
- Athens
- Thebes

</br>

### Sparta

**Hegemony attempts**

After the Peloponnesian war, Sparta's method of attempting to gain power was by dismantling democracies and demanding tribute from conquered cities. They established oligarchic regimes in these cities and appointed various government officials who were favourable to their ideology.

**Lysander as a god**

In order to do this, they sent General Lysander, who afterwards became worshipped as a sort of god - he had altars erected in his name, sacrifices made to him, and hymns composed for him. The Samians (of Samos) voted that their festival of Hera should be renamed 'Lysandreia'.

**The Persians**

Sparta also had the task of defending the Greek cities in Asia Minor against possible Persian incursions. They attempted to sponsor a Persian war, but Athens and Thebes had no desire to become entangled in such a conflict.

**Succession**

A succession struggle also broke out in Sparta, which was not aided by the prophesy of an oracle that the Spartans should 'beware a lame king'. Leotychides and Agesilaus were the contenders for the monarchy, with Agesilaus winning out the struggle (with the argument that 'lame' was a metaphor for 'not from royal stock' rather than physically disabled).

**The Corinthian War**

Sparta became engaged in a war with an alliance of the Boeotians, Athenians, Corinthians, and Argives, who intended to overthrow the Spartan regime. However, nothing follows from this war (even though it was the largest land battle ever fought in Greece), and it is ended by the 'king's peace': the Persian king Artaxerxes desired the cities in Asia as well as Clazomenae and Cyprus, vowing to wage war on whichever party did not accept to his terms (the peace favoured Sparta as it ruled out Athens' ability to make allies or form a league).

**Consolidation**

Sparta resolved to crack down on those cities which had been favourable towards the alliance during the Corinthian war, preventing them from making allies and forcing them to tear down their walls. They also required that men be sent for a combined armed forces (with heavy fines for non-compliance).

</br>

### Athens

Athens quickly regained their democracy through a resistance movement that fought against the thirty tyrants, with support from Thebes (who had originally been allied with Sparta against Athens during the war).

**A New Democracy**

Various constitutional changes were made. The assembly was originally led by a 'Council Leader' (which changed every month, from one of the ten tribes); this remained the same, but the council leader now had nothing to do with the assembly, removing power from an individual - instead this task was performed by the 9 Prohedroi.

Athens also started paying people to encourage participation in politics, by offering financial incentives. Previously the pay for voting in the assembly was one obol, but it was increased to three.

**The Walls**

Athens started to rebuild their walls (now named the Walls of Konon, Konon being an Athenian navy general) in order to make Athens more defensible after losing their walls as a result of the Spartan victory in the Peloponnesian war. A contractor from Thebes was used for the delivery of the stones.

**Another Persian War**

It was proposed that Athens and her allies fight another Persian war.


> [!quote] Isocrates, *Panegyricus* 166 & 187 (380 BCE)
> "it is much more glorious to fight against the King for his empire than to contend against each other for the hegemony"

**Another Empire?**

It was also proposed to Athens' old allies and others that Athens was open to a new alliance, on different terms to the old constitution: no tribute would be required, all cities could remain autonomous, etc.

</br>

### Thebes

**Boeotian Federalism**

All residents of an area were arranged into eleven divisions, each providing a *boeotarch*. Thebes contributed four, Orchomenus, Hysiae, Thespiae, Eutresis and Thisbae provided two, and Tanagra, Haliartus, Lebadea, Coronea, Acraephnium, Copae, and Chaeronea all sent one.

The Thebans claimed the right to swear the oath provided by Sparta on behalf of all Boeotians and argued that the 'independence clause' was not explained to them and so they would not sign it.

**The Boeotian War**

Sparta declared Thebes to be hostile, and began the Boeotian war against them. There is an attempt to make peace: the Thebans signed their names on the oath, but the next day demanded that the text be changed to read 'the boeotians' rather than 'the Thebans'. Agesilaus refused to do this, threatening to strike their names from the treaty.

During the Battle of Leuktra, 400 Spartans fell - this was a large proportion of their military, and demonstrated the dwindling population of the Spartan state. This was argued to be a result of the oligarchic system that Sparta employed - people were leaving as a result of having very little, whilst so much was owned by so few.

**The Thebans and the Persians**

The Thebans, with a view to Greek hegemony, decided to attempt to negotiate with the Persian king. Also in attendance at the court of the king were various other large and small city-states, all with the same intent. Thebes, however, was by far the largest and most powerful by this point.

**After the Battle of Mantineia (362 BC)**
 
The battle was supposed to be the final decisive chapter in the conflict. Each party claimed that they were victorious. However, none was found to be better off than another - no additional territory had been gained, no allegiances sworn, many combatants had perished, and Greece saw further confusion and discord than previously.
