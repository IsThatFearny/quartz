### *Agamemnon Chronology*

- The watchman sees the beacon lit and proclaims victory at Troy (Ilium)- Clytaemestra does likewise (letting out a 'cry of victory')
- A herald arrives, proclaiming the greatness of Agamemnon and Menelaus
- Clytaemestra reveals that Orestes is being raised by another for fear of plots against him in his father's absence
- Agamemnon returns, and briefly quarrels with Clytaemestra about how she should praise him; he emphasises that he remains mortal and thus should not be lauded as she does, but she convinces him that if Priam (king of Troy) had won such a victory, he would be treated the same - Agamemnon should accept honour
- Agamemnon introduces Cassandra, his Trojan concubine gifted to him by the army
- Cassandra refuses to exit the carriage despite encouragement from Clytaemestra - when the chorus convince her to, she steps out and, amid cries of appeal and questioning to Apollo, prophesises the death of Agamemnon by his wife's hand, at first cryptically and then explicitly
- Cassandra prays for her own swift death
- Agamemnon cries out, and the chorus discuss their opinions on their next course of action - it is decided that they will go to discover the plight of Agamemnon
- Agamemnon and Cassandra are found dead, with Clytaemestra standing over them, clothes stained with blood and sword in hand
- The chorus condemn Clytaemestra's actions, stating that she shall be exiled from the city
- Clytaemestra paradoxically denies her actions, stating simultaenously that she is not Agamemnon's murderer nor is she his spouse
- Aegisthus is introduced, providing the reasoning for the murder of Agamemnon - Atreus served Aegisthus' father (and Atreus' brother), Thyestes,the flesh of his own children, under the pretence that it was animal meat (this is omitted from *Agamemnon*, but Thyestes was having an adulterous relationship with Atreus' wife) - Thyestes places a curse on the house of Pelops: 'So perish all the race of Pleisthenes!'
- Aegisthus takes responsibility for the murder of Agamemnon
- Aegisthus orders the murder of the Chorus for the purported insolence toward him - this is halted by Clytaemestra
