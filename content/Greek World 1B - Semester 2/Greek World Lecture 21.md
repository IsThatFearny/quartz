### Alexandria

- Founded 331 BC
- City was built on pre-existing foundations of an ancient Egyptian city called Rhakotis
- Built around a grid-plan
- Citizens exempt from the poll tax which was enforced on other inhabitants of Egypt - Alexandria was a Greek city in the middle of the Egyptian state

</br>

### Tombs

- Alexander died in Babylon, and his body was taken by Ptolemy to Memphis and then brought to Alexandria, where it was entombed
- There are also various tombs of the Ptolemies, built out of Alabaster, a material which was unique to Alexandria (other Greek tombs tended to be built out of marble or similar rocks)
- The tumulus of the Ptolemies was largely Macedonian in design

</br>

### Pharos (lighthouse) of Alexandria

- One of the seven wonders of the ancient world
- The tallest tower in the ancient world, 120-140m tall
- Nothing survives aside from a handful of foundation blocks
- We have it depicted on ancient coins, giving us an idea of its shape and features
- Ascribed to Sostratos of Rhodes, who was credited with either designing it, dedicating it, or perhaps both
- Clearly, Greek technology used to build the lighthouse (as in Rhodes where the colossus stood)
- Blocks and sculptures have been found in the waters nearby which are believed to have belonged to the Pharos

</br>

### Serapis

- Serapis (to whom there is a temple at Alexandria) is a deity who combined Egyptian and Greek theology
- He was partly inspired by Osiris (Egyptian) and partly by Hades (Greek)
- He was worshipped at the Serepeion, along with his wife Isis
- It is thought that the temple of Serapis also served as a place of worship for the Ptolemies (inferred from inscriptions on the Altar of Serapis)

</br>
