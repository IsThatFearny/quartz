### Why division?

The empire was divided as a response to the difficulties in managing the empire as a result of its vast geographical extent

- A region would *secede* if the central authority was unable to ensure their security
- The imperial administration would *divide* the empire in order to provide a quicker reaction to crises

**N.B.** Despite division, a common Roman identity and community are *never lost*

The four Augusti and Caesares under Diocletian were assigned  operational spheres, rather than territories to rule. They frequently take action also outside their immediate areas (e.g. Galerius in Syria and Egypt). Diocletian’s supremacy avoids any possible conflict of competences, spheres of influence, or authority.

Constantine's three sons agreed to rule together, each with their own territories and with equal authority. However, the absence of supremacy between them fosters competition and ultimately causes a civil war.

### Valentinian

- Jovian acclaimed Augustus upon the death of Julian (June 363 CE) but reigns only until he dies of natural causes in February 364 CE
- **Valentinian I:** Born in Pannonia; successful career under Constantius; acclaimed as Augustus in Nicaea upon the death of Jovian
- Valentinian appointed his brother Valens as joint Augustus in March 364 CE
- Valentinian and Valens ruled jointly, each with their own sphere of influence: Valentinian in Trier, Valens in Antioch; Valentinian always maintained supremacy
- Valentinian's older son **Gratian** was proclaimed Augustus for the West in 367 CE, aged 8, in order to ensure a smooth succession
- Valentinian died of a stroke during a military campaign in 375 CE - the other emperors were too far away to intervene quickly, and the local high command claimed to need an emperor and proclaim Valentinian's younger son, Valentinian II (aged 4) despite the fact that Gratian held the position (the army were concerned that Gratian was not massively keen on them)

A fundamental change in imperial rule came after Gratian's death, when the officers of the *comitatus*, the empress Justina, and a council of generals begin to take decisions which were enforced through the Augustus' authority

**The End of the Dynasty**

- 9 August 378: the emperor Valens dies fighting against the Goths (Battle of Adrianople)

- 379: Gratian, now senior Augustus (aged 20), appoints the general Theodosius as colleague in order to deal with the Gothic problem

- 379-383: Three emperors (Gratian and Valentinian II in Trier, then Milan, Theodosius in Thessalonica then Constantinople). Valens, Gratian, and Valentinian have no sons: Theodosius starts a dynastic policy, bestowing the imperial title on his two sons. 

- 383: Gratian is killed by the usurper Magnus Maximus. The 12-year-old Valentinian II is now senior Augustus, but Theodosius is the most powerful figure by far. 

- 392: Valentinian is found dead, Theodosius remains sole ruler.

</br>

### Theodosius

- Born in c. 346/7 at Cauca in Spain, son of a leading general under Valentinian I, followed his father in his military career

- Retired to his Spanish estates after his father was executed in 376 – but recalled in 378 after (or before?) the Battle of Adrianople 

- Appointed Magister Militum (probably) by Gratian, assuming command in Illyricum 

- Proclaimed Augustus in the East 19 January 379

</br>

#### Religious Policy

- Theodosius adopted a policy of forced conversion to Christianity
- 380 CE - the Edict of Thessalonica is the closest it came to a proclamation of Christianity as the official religion of the empire
- 391 CE - The celebration of Pagan public rituals is prohibited

</br>

#### Dealing with the Goths

- Gothic groups enter the Roman Empire in 376 CE, asking for resettlement
- A series of small revolts gathers momentum and transforms into the **Gothic War**
- Valens was defeated at Adrianople in 376 CE
- Made peace in 382 CE, with many Goths absorbed into the local populations, and some choosing to continue to fight for the Emperor
- The treaty allowed Goths to settle on Roman territory as allied peoples, keping their own laws and politics

</br>

#### The Last Division

- Theodosius died in Milan in 395 CE, leaving a united Christian empire
- Said empire was to be ruled by his two sons, Arcadius in Constantinople and Honorius in Milan
- Arcadius was old enough to rule (18) and Honorius was under the guardianship of Theodosius' most loved general
- Neither emperor were interested in ruling, and actual government decisions were made by the court officers
