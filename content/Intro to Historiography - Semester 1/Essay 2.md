# Essay 2


## Assess the impact of postcolonial perspectives on the history of photography

Scholarship of the history of photography prior to the advent of postcolonial historiography and the concept of 'Orientalism' traditionally maintained a focus on technological progress, the science behind the camera, and photography as a worthy successor to the canvas. Such histories had a tendency to err heavily on the side of a eurocentric narrative - images of a stereotypical British Victorian family sitting and staring blankly into the camera spring to mind. However, the popularisation of 'orientalism' by Edward Said in his book of the same name introduced new, postcolonial perspectives to the history of photography. More recent scholarship has seen photography redefined as a tool of imperialist projects and a medium through which the concept of the exotic 'Orient' was promoted in Western circles, as well as promoting a novel view of photography as being practised and moulded by indigenous populations, not just the European photographers whom were often the sole focus of histories of photography in colonised regions. This essay shall argue that postcolonial perspectives have revolutionised the history of photography and introduced a new paradigm to the field, through analysis of orientalist tropes which photography reinforced, the practise of photography as a means of benefitting colonialist designs, the active participation of the subjects of orientalist photography, and the use of the colonial archive to shape future conceptions of the 'orient'.
