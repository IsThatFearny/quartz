### Part I

**Why does Rosenwein think emotions are important for the study of history?**

Political and economic events are not entirely unemotional affairs, and it is important to be able to attache emotion to these moments in order to understand motivations, causes, effects, etc. Fevre: "the emotional life is always ready to overflow the intellectual life". Fevre argued that history could not be understood nor be made to be understood "without this primordial interest that I call the psychological".


**What does Rosenwein mean by the 'hydraulic model' of emotions?**

The 'hydraulic model' is the historical view that describes emotions as "liquids" or "energies" which are pressurised and ready to release themselves. These can be suppressed, but if released they will lead to emotional outbursts. Rosenwein states that her models have superseded this.

**According to Rosenwein, what did Norbert Elias argue with respect to emotions in *The Civilising Process*? What impact, according to Rosenwein, has it had upon historical approaches to emotion?**

Elias welcomes emotionology, seeing it as a driving force behind understanding his 'civilising process'. Rosenwein: "it was in the psychology of the courtiers... that the civilising process first began". Elias' text has had a significant impact upon those studying the history of emotion, causing them to see the narrative of the West as a "history of increasing emotional restraint". This not only affected historians, but also those such as Weber, Freud, and Foucault

**What source material does she mention? What approaches to this material does she outline?**

Rosenwein refers to various primary sources: letters, diaries, court records, etc, as well as philosophical works and religious writings. She outlines multiple approaches, such as:

- viewing emotions as socially constructed
	- Emotions are not universal but are rather socially and culturally constructed, shaped by social norms
	- Historians must be careful not to be take an anachronistic approach when looking at past socieities
- emotions as 'cultural practices'
	- Historians should view emotions as cultural practices which are learn and practiced within specific historical and social contexts
	- emphasises the idea that emotional expression and regulation vary
- emotional communities
	- groups of people who share common emotional practices and norms
	- defined by religion, social class, gender, etc.
- private vs public emotions
- interdisciplanary
