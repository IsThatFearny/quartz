### Early Women's History

- Activists (feminists and the women's rights movements) looking to the past to understand the present
- 'First wave feminism' - women missing from historical scholarship in late 19<sup>th</sup> and early 20<sup>th</sup> century. Women therefore wrote their own studies of women's work, unionism and politics
- Suffrage campaigners published their accounts of the demand for the vote which remain influential for researches
- Early histories had no impact on the established history curriculum

</br>

### 'Second Wave Feminist' history

- Second Wave Feminism or the Women's Liberation Movement - rediscovering women's role in the past, again
- Situated in the context of development of social, labour, oral history, as well as history from below. Also important is the growth of the social sciences and sociology
- Challenged the focus on economic and political history
- No just putting women back into History, but expanding and rethinking the way that it was written

</br>

### Growth and development of Women's History

- Growth in the US and Britain, with difference emphasis on certain aspects:
	- US - separate women's culture, growth of all female institutions, family, and sexuality
	- Britain - waged work, union organisation, and labour politics
- Critiques of dominance of white, western heterosexual women and their concerns - no considerations of race, ethnicity, class and sexual orientation
- Boundaries between different approaches becomes more fluid over time
