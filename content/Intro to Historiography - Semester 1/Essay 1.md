### Is 'history from below' a reflection of the theories of Marx and Engels?

</br>

Whilst 'history from below' has its roots in pre-Marxist scholarship, its actual genesis was heavily influenced by the theories of Marx and Engels.[^1] Scholars such as E.P. Thompson, Eric Hobsbawm, and Raphael Samuel all had a Marxist or Communist background, and practised history using Marxist methodology. However, modern 'history from below' or 'people's history' has developed significantly since the scholarship of the mid-20<sup>th</sup> century and has diverged significantly in both methodology and ethos. One cannot therefore argue that 'history from below' remains true to its Marxist roots as its modern form has evolved too far to remain identifiable with Marxism, and this essay contends this position.

'History from Below' was indeed the brainchild of Marxist scholarship in the post-war years: the History Workshop at Ruskin College and the Fabian Society were major participants in its development. The works of the

Marx saw 'history from below' as a method of studying History that would allow its practitioner to understand societal change and class struggle. The primary focus of this form of history was the 'working class' (although Marx never used this term), and thus it was a majoritarian study. Early Marxist scholars often spoke of 'people's history' as synonymous with 'labour history' or, as Thompson himself spoke of, 'history-as-industrial relations'.[^2] Given the Marxist focus on the revolutionary transition from Capitalism to Socialism, this makes sense: the proletariat were understood to be the primary agents in bringing this about. However, late-19<sup>th</sup> Century scholarship often makes the criticism that 'history from below' in this form focuses too much on class and collective agency, and tends to ignore individual characteristics such as gender, age, race, and individual agency. These criticisms led to significant development in the field: the modern form of 'people's history' distances itself from the focus on class struggle and the proletariat. Whilst still majoritarian, the majority is no longer identifiable with class or economic ideologies.
