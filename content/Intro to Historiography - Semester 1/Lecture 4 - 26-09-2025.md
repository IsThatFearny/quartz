### Adam Smith

- Now known mainly as an economist
- Associated with
	- Belief in free markets and a minimal role for government
	- 'invisible hand' (regulation of econ. activity)
	- advocacy of economic self-interest
- Smith's beliefs were more complex than this, and certain agents have cherry-picked aspects of his work
- Key concern was with explaining historical change and development of society, including their economic development, although Smith started with human nature rather than economics
- Primary work was *Wealth of Nations*; also wrote a second book, *The Theory of Moral Sentiments*

</br>

### Das Adam-Smith-Problem

- Formulated by German economic historians in the 19<sup>th</sup> century, arguing that different views of human nature underpinned each book
- Now widely dismissed: there is no break between the two works: whilst there is some difference in the subject matter, both share the same view of human nature

</br>

### Commercial society and the division of labour

- Commercial society generates more wealth for everyone than previous stages
- The poor in these societies enjoy more comfort and prosperity than the wealthy in earlier, less advanced stages of society. Why? **Because specialisation requires commercialisation to acquire everything to meet one's needs**
- Division of labour begins between agriculturalists and manufacturers, leading to increases in agricultural productivity. This is followed by division within each of these sectors (but especially in manufacturing)
