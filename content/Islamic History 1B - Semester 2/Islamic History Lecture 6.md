### Saladin

Whilst Nur al-Din and his father Zengi were the first muslims to seriously fight the Crusaders, Saladin conquered Jerusalem and was seen as the real hero, especially in Frankish lands. Whilst he had a rocky relationship with Reynald of Chatillon, vowing to kill him personally, he was views as the 'ideal knight' by Muslims and Christians alike.

Saladin's legacy is present in much Muslim and Christian contemporary imagery, such as his eagle being used in the coat of arms of Egypt and of the Kurdistani regional government, his image used in a stamp of Saddam Hussein (who tried to depict himself as a second Saladin), and Kaiser Wilhelm II's bronze wreath in honour of Saladin.

### Women during the Crusades

There were various women who ruled in the Middle East:

- Queen Eleanor of Aquitaine (r. 1137-1204) - led a crusader campaign during the second crusade
- Queen Melisende (r. 1131-1152) - queen of Jerusalem
- Queen SIbylla (r. 1186-1190) - defended Jerusalem against Saladin's siege in 1187
- etc.

Perhaps the most important female leader was al-Malika Asmat ad-Din Umm Khalil Shadjar al-Durr (henceforth, simply Shadjar al-Durr), who was briefly Sultan of Egypt from May-July 1250, and ruled as regent from Nov. 1249-Feb. 1250.

</br>

### Transitioning from Ayyubid to Mamluk Rule

- In 1249, the Ayyubid Sultan of Egypt, al-Salih Ayyub, died - his wife hid his death to avoid a succession struggle
- Shadjar al-Durr defended Egypt from the armies of the seventh crusade (1248-1254)
- Al-Salish Ayyub's son, Turanshah, was declared the Sultan of Egypt, however was killed by slaves in 1250 (likely instigated by Shadjar al-Durr) - the last non-Mamluk commander of the Ayyubid army was also killed
- In 1250, Shadjar al-Durr was declared Sultana (Queen of the Muslims), and she was the first Sultan of the Mamluks; the Atabeg (tutor) of the Ayyubid prince was declared commander of the Mamluks
- A descendant of the Ayyubids of Yeme and a child is declared 'nominal' Ayyubid Sultan of Egypt
