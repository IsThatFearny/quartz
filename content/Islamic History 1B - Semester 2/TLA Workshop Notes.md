# TLA Workshop Notes

</br>

- Questions released at 10am on Wednesday 9th April
- Must be submitted on Wednesday 16th April by 4pm
- Completed in exam conditions (i.e. no tutor contact)
- Should be possible to complete in a few hours (check exceptional circumstances here, clashes with three days of Finals)
- Choice of four questions for part A; choice of three sources for part B
- Altogether one needs to write 1500 words; parts A and B count for equal amounts
- Each part should be 750 words max

</br>

### Section A

- Address the question
- If the question is narrowed down, be explicit about focus and provide a good rationale for doing so
- Answer with an argument - no narrative
- Substantiate with accurate and relevant information, context, explanation, analysis, etc.
- Relate broad concepts to specific examples, where appropriate
- Structure like a mini essay - short introduction, a structured body, and a short conclusion
- Avoid direct quotations from literature, although scholars can be referred to by name when relevant
- No footnotes or bibliography are required

</br>

### Section B

- Discuss one primary source - offered 4 to choose from
- If the author of the source is known, it will be a previously read author
- Situate the primary source in its historical context - who wrote the source, when and where was the source written, what was going on at the time, etc.
- Analyse the primary source:
	- What is the author's key message? Is it explicit or implicit?
	- How does the author try to get the message across? Do they substantiate their points? What assumptions underlay the message?
	- Does the text convey a certain tone? How does the language work? What are the important metaphors and symbols? What can the author's choice of words tell you?
	- What is the author's point of view? How does the author's point of view relate to their background?
	- What is the author's potential bias? What does the author choose to talk and not talk about? Whose points of view are left out?
- Whilst direct quotations are discouraged, words from the text can be used in order to emphasise a point about language or tone
- No footnotes or bibliography, but it is encouraged to cite certain authors (including their death date if they are contemporary to the time)

</br>
