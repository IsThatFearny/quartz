### Succession

The first four caliphs were all descendants of the Prophet. The Umayyad dynasty had a common ancestor in Umayyah, and the Abbasids in Abbas. 

There was no rule for succession beyond familial relations to the prophet. After Ali was murdered in 661, the Muslim community split into two factions with differing views about succession:

- Shi'at Uthman (Party of Uthman): Caliphs should be chosen from among the tribe of the Prophet, the Quraysh
- Shi'at Ali (Party of Ali): Ali should have become the first Caliph, and only members of the Prophet's immediate family should become Caliph

Shi'ism developed into a distinctive branch, leading rebellions against the Umayyad and Abbasid caliphates, but were never able to hold power.

</br>

### The Development of Sunnism

The Sunni doctrine developed in oppositions to Shi'ism amongst religious scholars during the 9th-10th century. There were four Sunni schools which were decided to be the 'official' schools (though several others were founded): 

- The Hanafi school
- The Maliki school
- The Shafi'i school
- The Hanbali school

According to the Sunni doctrine, the whole Muslim community had to reach a consensus in order to choose a leader. Said leader must not necessarily be from the immediate family of the prophet, but must be a part of the Quraysh.
