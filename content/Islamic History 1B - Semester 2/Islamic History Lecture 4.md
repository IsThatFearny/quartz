### Terminology

- Crux = Cross = Crusade
- In Arabic, the crusades were termed *Al-hurub al-salibiyya*, 'the wars of the cross'
- Muslims termed Latin crusaders 'Franks' - *Franj, Ifranj*
- The crusader states were the Latin Christian states of the Levant/Outremer

</br>

### Context

- **1071:** The Great Seljuqs defeat the Byzantines at Manzikert, conquering Asia Minor
- **1074:** Pope Gregory VII calls for a campaign to liberate the Holy Sepulchre - this call was largely ignored

</br>

### Latin vs Byzantine sources

Latin sources claim that the First Crusade was a result of a call made to the Pope by Alexius I Comnenus for military aid against the Turks; however, Byzantine sources claim that Alexius had asked for help against the Pechenegs in the Balkans

Traditional historiography has the instigator of the crusades as Pope Urban II (r. 1088-1099). speaking at the Council of Clermont and encouraging Christians to fight for the holy land.

Latin sources describe a strong popular response to Urban's speeches, with the contingent being led largely by middle-ranking nobles. Knights were generally skilled in fighting and desired the opportunity for redemption. The crusades are largely characterised as pilgrimages.


</br>

### Timeline

- **1095:** Urban II's preaching tour stops at a Church council in Clermont
- **1096:** First crusade armies arrive at Constantinople
- **1097:** Armies take Nicaea (the capital of the Rum Seljuq Sultanate), giving it to the Byzantine emperor. The Seljuqs pushed into the Anatolian plateau, and Konya becomes the new Rum Seljuq capital
- **1098:** Capture of Edessa and Antioch
- **1099:** Capture of Tripoli and Jerusalem

</br>

### The Crusader States

The success of the first crusade resulted in the creation of various 'crusader states':

- County of Edessa (1098-1144), controlled by the Baldwins from Flanders
- Principality of Antioch (1098-1268), controlled by Normans from Southern Italy
- County of Tripoli (1109-1289), controlled by the Raymonds from Southern France
- Kingdom of Jerusalem (1099-1291), controlled by the Baldwins from Flanders
