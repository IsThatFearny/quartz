### Islam as social and political idiom

**An urbanised society with diverse authorities**

- Abbasid Sunni caliphs as leaders of the Muslim community (*umma*)
- Shi'I Imams
- *ulama*: religious scholars as urban and local elites
- Sultans and Amirs/Emirs: 'warlords' turned military and political leaders of principalities and states

</br>

### Development of Islamic institutions

- *madhhabs*: schools of Islamic law
- *madrasas*: colleges, foundations of Islamic learning
- *waqf*: charitable trusts
- *ulama*
- *Sufis*: Ascetics and mystics, founders of Sufi orders
- Minorities/majorities: Jews, Christians, Zoroastrians, Shamanists, Buddhists, Hindus, etc.

</br>

### Religion and Society

- Sunnis
	- Samanids, Ghaznavids, Seljuqs, Zengids, Ayyubids, Mamluks of Egypt, Ilkhanids, Mughals, etc.
- Twelver Shi'a
	- 12 generations of the descendants of the 4th caliph, Ali (r. 655-661) and his wife, and the Prophet's daughter Fatima
	- Buyids and Safavids in Iran
- Isma'ili Shi'a
	- Fatimid caliphs/imams in Egypt
	- Nizari Isma'ilis (Assassins) in Iran and Syria

</br>

### Nomadic Invasions

- 11th Century: Turkic people led by the Seljuq family invade the central islamic lands and establish the Great Seljuq Empire of Iran
- 13th Century: the Mongos invade central Islamic lands and establish the Ilkhanid Empire of Iran
- 14th Century: the Turco-Mongol Timurids invade the central Islamic lands and establish the Timurid empire
- The Great Seljuqs, Iran (1040-93) - Persian men of the Pen and Turkish men of the Sword

</br>

### The Seljuqs of Rum (1081-1307)

- Conquest of Byzantine/Eastern Roman (Rum) territory in Anatolia - rise of the Rum Seljuqs
- Urban Sunni Islam of the ruling Seljuq elite and syncretic Folk-Islam of their Turkmen followers in the periphery
- Continuation of steppe traditions: sovereignty as the possession of the leading family, not an autocratic ruler
- Migration of pastoral nomads and nomadisation of central Islamic lands and Anatolia

</br>

### The Mongol Peoples

- Mongol invasions led by Genghis Khan and his descendants
- Early Mongol rule in the Islamic World:
	- Pagan rulers
	- Legitimisation of power as Mongol conquerors
	- Allied to the Great Mongol Khan
	- Practised destruction and extortion of Muslim subjects
	- Nomadisation
- Later Mongol rulers:
	- Ilkhanids of Iran (1295-1388)
	- Conversion to Islam - Sufism played a major role
	- 1295-1304, the rule of SUltan Mahmud Ghazan Padshah-i Islam
	- Legitimisation of power as Muslim rulers, independent from the Great Khan
	- Iran and Anatolia incorporated into the Mongol world system, exchanging culture, knowledge, and goods
	- Gunpowder brought from China into the Islamic world
- Tamerlane (1369-1405)
	- Contradictions were part of his self-image, which was used for legitimisation
	- He was an exceptionally brutal conqueror but sponsored the arts and sciences
	- Massacred fellow Muslims and destroyed Muslim cities
	- Samarkand was built with wealth looted from sacked cities
	- Posed a serious threat to other Muslim states
