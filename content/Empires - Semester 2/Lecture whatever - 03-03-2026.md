- Pre-Atlantic routes: Asia -> 'Middle East' -> Mediterranean -> Western Europe
- Aristocratic consumption of sugar largely performative as a symbol of power
- Over time, sugar replaced honey as the dominant metaphor for sweetness, desire, etc.

### Plantation Slavery and the Atlantic

- Sugar plantations require a lot of land, intensive labour, and strict discipline
- Plantations built on the transatlantic slave trade
- Plantation success requires constant replenishment of labour where slaves are worked to exhaustion
- Constant expansion and overworking of a plantation would have environmental issues, destroying the topsoil for future planting
- Mass, consumable sugar is inextricably linked to slavery - the reduction of price was directly as a result of lower labour costs
- Sugar also key to ideas about purity and refinement - white sugar more expensive and seen as 'pure'

### Sugar, Tea, and the Provisioning of Labour

- Tea + Sugar considered a good industrial food
- Tea did not require cooking and provided quick calories, fitting the short breaks that industrial workers were permitted
- Sugar useful as a rapid energy source esp. for repetitive labour
- Sugar also used as 'grab-and-go' calories in the morning

### Time, Gender, and the Household

- Industrial capitalism reshaped meals as a result of fuel and time scarcity
- Women and children increasingly entering labour - as women were traditionally responsible for meals they needed to find meals that could fit around factory time
- Bread + Jam = Preservation and speed
