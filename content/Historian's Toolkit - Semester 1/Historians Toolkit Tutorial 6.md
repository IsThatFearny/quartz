### What does Peniston-Bird mean when she writes that the value of an interview may lie "not upon its adherence to fact, but precisely upon its departure from it"? What do you think of this claim? 

In this context, Peniston-Bird means that there is value to be found in claims that are perhaps inaccurate (such as Revill's dating of the foundation of the Home Guard). For example, Revill's insistence upon dating it accurately perhaps conveys the association of the Home Guard with the events of Dunkirk (even though it was not founded as a direct consequence). I think that this claim is fairly accurate - it is not often the purpose of an oral history/interview to glean entirely accurate information, but rather to gain an insight into the perspective of a certain individual, whatever their biases/prejudices may be.

### Why does Kennedy think that the oral history of lesbian communities (like the one from Buffalo that she gathered stories from) challenges conventional assumptions about Stonewall’s significance?


Kennedy argues that the mythology of Stonewall primarily involves a narrative of coming out and fighting back. However, she says that from many oral histories from various Lesbian communities that she has interviewed beg to differ: Lesbians lead the way in the 40s and 50s in being 'out' and heavily involved in activism. 

</br>

### In the oral history interview Virginia Apuzzo conveys forcefully that Stonewall _was_ transformative. How should we make sense of Apuzzo's view alongside Kennedy's more cautious interpretation of Stonewall’s significance?

Apuzzo's view is not incorrect, per se, but is perhaps exaggerative. An acknowledgement that gay life and activism existed in many forms pre-stonewall may be required in explaining the effects of stonewall. There is little doubt that Stonewall did change the mindset of many gays and lesbians in the Western World, but whether it was the first instance of LGBT 'activism' in the Western World is doubtful. As Kennedy shows, Lesbians (and perhaps by extension, gay men) indeed had their own world pre-stonewall and were involved in activism.
