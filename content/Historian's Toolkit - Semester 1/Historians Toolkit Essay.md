### # Do we distort LGBTQ+ history by treating the Stonewall Riots as a moment of radical change?

The Stonewall Riots of 1969 are widely agreed upon by leading historians to be a 'moment of radical change' in the trajectory of the LGBTQ+ rights movement.[^1] However, there is debate over whether, by treating the event as such, we 'distort' the history of what came before. This essay shall argue that treating Stonewall as a moment of radical change, does not necessarily distort pre-stonewall LGBTQ+ history, but we must avoid defining Stonewall as the 'beginning' of the LGBTQ+ rights movement.

There is clear evidence of Stonewall's transformative impact upon the LGBTQ+ rights movement. On the whole, the movement saw a major shift from the homophile movement's approach of legal challenges and peaceful demonstrations with an emphasis on single-issue politics, to the gay liberation movement's tactics of direct action (largely adopted from various other civil rights movements of the 1960s) and the politicisation of sexuality and 'coming out'.[^2] This change was marked by an explosion in the number of organisations post-Stonewall: just before Stonewall occured, there were around fifty LGBTQ+ rights organisations in the US; By 1973, this had grown to over eight-hundred.[^3] Various victories were won, such as out gays running for office and in some cases even winning, and the representation (although still relatively rare) of gay characters in media.[^4] The pre-Sto
