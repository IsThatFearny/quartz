### How differently have historians tried to explain why Stonewall happened and mattered so much to many marginalised people in 1969?

A popular interpretation (although of dubious credibility) is that Stonewall was simply a spontaneous mass eruption of fury and resistance. Those who propel this interpretation seek to argue that Stonewall was the beginning of the LGBT liberation movement, the first time that the LGBT community had fought back against oppression. 

However, many activists and historians have been often known to criticise this interpretation as a myth, and prefer to argue that the riots were the culmination of two decades of LGBT activism, stemming from oppression in the McCarthy era and WWII mobilisation. Homophile groups existed a long time before the Stonewall riots, and had achieved various victories, however small, such as the right to publish their periodicals. Stonewall is argued to simply have been a group of activists choosing to use the police raid as an excuse to riot. However, this interpretation is also widely criticised: the demographic of the bar's patrons were not activist types, and there is no clear correlation between gay activist movements and the rioters.

There is also a third interpretation of the cause of Stonewall was the use of anti-raid tactics in bars which had been established over the previous decades. During prohibition and in many gay bars, there were methods to prevent raids, such as paying off the police, using mafia protection (as Stonewall used), and developed early warning systems. Some historians see Stonewall as the culmination of these defence methods.
